## Executive Summary

The **Growth and Skills Levy** is the evolution of the apprenticeship levy, providing more flexibility to employers. Key change: **Apprenticeship Units** - short, flexible training courses that can be funded through the levy, launching **April 2026**.

---

## What is the Growth and Skills Levy?

**Definition:**
> "The Growth and Skills Levy provides a more flexible offer to employers and learners, building on the apprenticeship levy."

**Purpose:**
- Help people learn new, relevant high-quality skills at work
- Fuel innovation in businesses across the country
- Provide more flexibility than traditional apprenticeship model

## 🎯 KEY INNOVATION: Apprenticeship Units

### What Are Apprenticeship Units?

**Definition:**
- Short, flexible training courses
- Funded through the Growth and Skills Levy
- Designed to help employers respond quickly to evolving skills needs
- Allow investment in workforce development

### Focus Areas

**Critical Skills Areas:**

## Other Flexibilities in Growth and Skills Levy

### 1. English and Maths Requirements

**Change:**
- **Adult apprentices (19+):** No longer required to hold or achieve English and maths qualifications to pass apprenticeship
- **Instead:** Demonstrate English and maths skills by using them in real work tasks
- **16-18 year olds:** Still required to achieve English and maths qualifications
- **Funding:** Still available for English and maths qualifications where employer agrees

### 2. Minimum Duration

**Change:**
- **Reduced from 12 months to 8 months** (from August 2025)
- **Condition:** Only where it is right to do so
- **Rationale:** Allows apprentices to achieve occupational competence more quickly where it makes sense (e.g., significant prior learning, better alignment with industry)
- **Note:** Typical duration for each apprenticeship still exists

### 3. Assessment Model Changes

**Streamlined Process:**
- Assessments more proportionate to competency being tested
- Removes duplication
- Assessment plans can allow assessments to take place during programme
- Training providers can deliver elements of assessment
- End-point Assessment Organisations (EPAOs) continue to shape assessment and ensure validity

**Implementation:**
- Changes apply to all apprenticeships, at all levels
- Existing assessment plans being rewritten standard-by-standard
- Foundation apprenticeships already developed in line with new assessment plans

### 4. Level 7 Apprenticeships

**Change (From 1 January 2026):**
- **Focus on young people:** Only Level 7 apprenticeships for 16-21 year olds (and under 25 for care leavers and those with EHCP) eligible for government funding
- **22+ year olds:** Not eligible for Level 7 funding
- **Grandfathering:** Any apprentice who started Level 7 before 1 January 2026 continues to be funded through to completion

**Rationale:**
> "The government is refocusing investment towards young people at the start of their working lives, rather than those already in work with higher levels of prior learning and qualifications."

### 5. New Investment

**Autumn Budget Announcement:**
- Package of additional investment for next phase of Growth and Skills Levy
- Ensures skills to power economic growth
- Rolls out new flexibilities for business
- Delivers important reforms
- Supports young people to progress more quickly into skilled, well-paid jobs

### 6. Key Changes (Future)

**From Next Academic Year:**
- Fully funded apprenticeships for non-levy payers (mainly SMEs) available for eligible under 25 year olds

**From 2026/27 Academic Year:**
- Remove additional 10% top up to account funds
- Change expiry window for levy funds from 24 months to 12 months
- Change government's co-investment rate to 75% (from 95%) for levy-paying employers once they have exhausted all their funds
  - This means levy-paying employers co-investment rate will be 25% if they have used all funds in their account

**Streamlining:**
- Work with employers and Skills England to streamline range of apprenticeship standards available

## Growth and Skills Levy Products

### 1. Apprenticeships

**Traditional Model:**
- Real job enabling individuals to earn while they learn
- Gain valuable knowledge, skills and behaviours tailored to specific job role
- Apprentices have contract with employer
- Allows employers to invest in future by bringing in talent that fits business needs

### 2. Foundation Apprenticeships

**Introduced:** 1 August 2025

**Characteristics:**
- **Opportunities for young people:** Paid jobs with structured training in entry level occupation (Level 2)
- Designed for young people at start of their career
- **Pathway to progression:** Clear progression route
- Enable individuals to gain relevant employability skills and experience
- Can lead to more specific or advanced apprenticeships
- Supports long-term career development and growth

**Sectors (First Wave):**
- Construction and the built environment
- Engineering and manufacturing
- Digital
- Health and social care

**Standards:** Published on Skills England's website

### 3. Apprenticeship Units

**New Product - Launching April 2026:**
- Short, flexible training courses
- Funded through Growth and Skills Levy
- Designed to help employers respond quickly to evolving skills needs
- Investment in workforce development
- Available in critical skills areas (digital, advanced AI, engineering)
- Designed to be affordable alongside wider apprenticeship offer
