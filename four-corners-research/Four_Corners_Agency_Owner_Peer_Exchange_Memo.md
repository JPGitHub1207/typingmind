# Four Corners for agency owners: should this become a business?

**Commercial decision memo**  
14 August 2026  
Target: UK Marketing / PR agency owners

Evidence labels: **Verified** · **Calculated** · **Estimated / assumption** · **Unknown**

Pricing, membership counts and sponsor fees appear only where a public source exists, or are marked as estimates. ONS counts are control-rounded to base 5.

---

**TOTAL ADDRESSABLE AGENCY OWNERS:** **23,640** VAT/PAYE enterprises in SIC 73.11 (advertising agencies) + 70.21 (PR and communication). **Verified**, ONS UK business 2025, March 2025. Most are 0–4 employee micro-firms.  
**REALISTIC SERVICEABLE MARKET:** **~1,700–2,200** independent, founder-active agencies with enough scale to expense peer support and not so large they already have a board. **Calculated** from ONS 10–49 employment (1,765) and £1–10m turnover (2,160), then **estimated** ~20–30% leakage to networks / hired MDs / non-owners.  
**RECOMMENDED INITIAL SEGMENT:** Independent **15–40 person**, roughly **£1–5m fee income**, founder still MD.  
**RECOMMENDED PRICE:** Do not launch. If you ignore that: **£3,500–£5,000/year** employer-paid. Not £50/session. Not Vistage money.  
**RECOMMENDED GROUP SIZE:** **8**  
**RECOMMENDED ECONOMIC ENGINE:** **Do not build one in this vertical.** If forced: **member-paid peer boards**. Not sponsors. Not events. Not benchmarking.  
**YEAR-1 REALISTIC REVENUE RANGE:** **£0** (recommended). If launched against advice: **£40,000–£90,000**.  
**YEAR-3 REALISTIC REVENUE RANGE:** **£150,000–£350,000** as a specialist practice. Not a scaled company. Agency Hackers already occupies the scaled version.

**Verdict:** the owner problem is real. The product is already on sale. **Do not launch a Four Corners-style peer exchange for UK marketing and PR agency owners.**

## 1. Executive Summary

This is not a white-space idea. It is a late copy of a business that has been running since 2017.

**Agency Hackers** sells, as a published membership:

- Standard **£250 per month** (Large agency 50+ people: **£450 per month**)
- An **Agency Board**: 10–12 agency leaders, matched by stage and scale, face-to-face **four times a year**, chaired by a seasoned agency leader
- Weekly online case studies, Slack/WhatsApp, 700+ videos

That is the Corner. It is already priced, packaged and sold to the exact human you would call.

In May 2025 Ian Harris stated that Agency Hackers is **running The Agency Collective alongside Agency Hackers**. TAC already claimed **nearly 500** agency leaders and published tiers from **£99 to £999 per month**. The two closest UK substitutes are now one operator.

**Wow Company BenchPress** already owns independent-agency benchmarking (2025: Gross Profit on £1m+ agencies **below 40% for the first time**, **46%** cite winning new business as the top challenge, **58%** say selling the business is their number one priority).

**Vistage, TAB, EO and Helm** already sell cross-industry owner boards to anyone whose agency is large enough to pay.

So the strategic question is not “do agency owners need peers?” They do. The question is “can a new Four Corners take money for a 8–12 person confidential group without being a weaker Agency Hackers?”

**No — not as a company worth incorporating.** The pain is not the constraint. Distribution and switching cost are. A founder with a London address book can run **one** paid board as a lifestyle practice. That is coaching with extra chairs. It is not a Four Corners business.

If investing my own money I would **kill this vertical** and, if I still wanted an owner-peer firm, run a separate kill-test on **recruitment-agency owners** (18,220 placement agencies, no Agency Hackers equivalent found in this research). I would not quietly pivot the Four Corners L&D brand into agencies. Different buyer, different competitors, different ethics.

## 4. Owner Pain-Point Analysis

### Verified

From **Wow BenchPress 2025** (£1m+ report headlines on Wow’s site):

1. Winning new business is the #1 challenge for **46%** (highest since 2012).
2. Profitability **19%**.
3. Working in the business not on it **12%**.
4. Cash flow **10%**.
5. Recruitment **5%**.
6. Gross Profit **below 40%** for the first time; **24%** hit the ~50% GP target.
7. **57%** grew fee income (historically 75–80% in a “normal” year).
8. **33%** made redundancies (26% in 2023).
9. **58%** say selling the business is their number one priority (44% two years earlier).
10. **95%** use AI; only **26%** have an AI strategy.
11. London agencies: **5%** lower GP and OP than elsewhere because salary inflation outruns charge-out.
12. Under-£1m BenchPress (Scribd copy of Wow report): GP **36%**, OP **12%**, flat on 2023.

From **Moore Kingston Smith 2025**:

- Average GI growth **3.4%** vs ~9.4% outlook earlier in the year.
- Digital agencies **−6.5%** revenue; PR a rare revenue decline.
- OP **10.2%**; PR **13.7%** and **no PR consultancy in the survey reported a loss**.
- Media buyers **+12.5%**.

From **IPA Census 2025**: member headcount **−6.8%**; creative/non-media **−14.3%**; churn up; vacancies down.

From **PRCA Census 2025**: 478 respondents; **39%** London; AI anxiety; wellbeing inequalities. This is a workforce census, not an owner P&L census.

### Commonly claimed / anecdotal (do not treat as measured)

Founder burnout, late payment, scope creep, freelancer dependence, procurement, “pitching for free”, client concentration. All plausible. **No primary statistic in this research quantifies prevalence for UK agency owners as a class**, except where they sit inside “new business” and “cash flow” above.

### Top 10 with strongest evidence

1. New business drought (Wow 46%).
2. Gross profit collapse vs 50% target (Wow).
3. Fee-income stagnation (57% grew; MKS 3.4%).
4. Digital more hurt than PR (MKS).
5. Exit obsession without a saleable machine (Wow 58%).
6. Cash buffer thinning (Wow 23% have >6 months overheads).
7. Redundancy as a normal tool (33%).
8. London cost/price squeeze (Wow).
9. AI used without a strategy (95% vs 26%).
10. Working in not on the business (12% name it #1; likely under-stated).

These are **P&L and pipeline** problems. They are not “I need a community” problems. A Corner only wins if it is a **decision room for those ten**, not a networking club.

## 6. Agency Hackers Deep Dive

**Verified from agencyhackers.com, 14 Aug 2026.**

- Audience: independent agency owners **and** leaders. “You don’t have to be the owner.” Suppliers cannot join as members; they go to partnerships.
- Standard **£250/month**; Large (50+ people) **£450/month**. Annual card/DD; they will take annual if you email. **No minimum term.**
- Agency Board: **10–12**, **quarterly F2F**, matched to stage/scale, chaired by a seasoned agency leader.
- 15–30 online sessions per quarter; 700+ archive; Slack and WhatsApp; topical working groups (AI, new business); Leadership Programme (quarterly F2F + monthly online) **included**.
- Live events open to non-members with member discounts. Ibiza Super Summit (23 Sep 2026) described as **200** agency leaders. London programmed (Agency Excellence, Robots Are Coming, Transform, Clockwork).
- Recommended partners (verified list): Synergist, ALF, Friday Solved, Hosting.com, Smile Digital Talent, Supo, Tend Legal, **The Wow Company**, co.agency, Prodigi. Event sponsors exist; a member testimonial on the homepage mentions being **invited to dinner by a sponsor**.
- Head of Partnerships Amy Moore (LinkedIn): previously TAC, now AH; partnerships@ — commercial engine is **membership + events + partners**.
- Founded 2017; LinkedIn company page 10–20 employees.
- **Agency Collective:** Ian Harris, 19 May 2025: “we’re now running The Agency Collective… grow it alongside Agency Hackers.” Peter Czapp (Wow) becomes Head of Insights at AH.

**Does AH already solve the Four Corners job?** For “curated, repeating, facilitated peer group of agency owners” — **yes, as a line-item inside a broader community.** Gaps they leave:

1. Boards are **quarterly**, not 6–8 weekly. Some owners want more cadence. Unknown how many.
2. Membership is **noisy** (Slack, 25 sessions, Ibiza). Owners who hate community product may still want a silent board.
3. Not owner-only. MDs and senior team can join. Confidentiality about equity, divorce, sale, and under-the-table pay is weaker if the person opposite is a Head of Ops not a shareholder.
4. Financial KPI sharing is **not** described as mandatory.
5. Supplier dinners happen around the brand.

Those gaps are real and **narrow**. They justify a boutique of 2–3 groups. They do not justify a company.

**Do not copy them.** You cannot out-archive 700 videos or out-party Ibiza.

## 9. Group Matching

**Primary key: size/stage.** A 6-person PR shop and a 70-person integrated agency do not have the same cash, hiring or exit problems.

**Secondary: geography for F2F.** London; else online.

**Tertiary: non-overlapping client sectors** so two PR firms in financial services are not in the same room.

**Do not mix as equals:**

- PR retainers vs performance/media (different gross-margin maths; MKS shows media +12.5%, digital −6.5%, PR rare decline).
- Independent vs network subsidiary.
- Owner vs employed MD (unless the MD has P&L and equity).

**PR + brand + content + “marketing agency”** can share owner problems (pipeline, utilisation, people, cash). **Performance/media buying** should be a separate board if you ever have two groups.

**Optimum composition:** 8 independent founder-MDs, **15–40 staff**, mixed disciplines **except** two direct pitch competitors, London-accessible, similar fee-income band (£1–5m).

## 11. Sponsorship Economics

Suppliers **do** pay to reach agency owners. That is not in dispute.

**Verified:** AH recommended partners (agency management software, legal, recruitment, accountants, new-business data). AH events have sponsors; members get sponsor dinners. Wow is both benchmarker and accountant. Glenister **£10k+VAT** for 10–12 named guests. iVentiv sponsors sit in the room (wrong audience, right mechanism).

**Rational CLV (illustrative, not a survey):** an accountant to a £2m agency might bill **£8–25k/year**. A Synergist-class system might be **£5–20k/year**. Recruitment of one senior might yield **£8–15k** fee. **Unknown** actual. Enough that **£5–15k** for a hosted dinner is rational. **£2k for a logo on a pitch-free Corner is not.**

**Formats**

| Format | Sponsor will pay? | Trust |
|---|---|---|
| Outside the room, anonymised insight | Weak at n=8 | OK |
| In the room | Yes (Glenister/AH dinner) | This is access |
| Expert then leaves | Maybe | Medium |
| Large event only | Yes | Fine if events exist |
| Annual category | Yes if you have 100+ owners | You don’t |

**Can sponsorship subsidise peer groups without becoming access-selling?** At 8 people, **no**. The unit is too small for insight and too intimate for a guest. Either the sponsor is buying **names** or they are donating. Donations are not an engine.

AH already monetises the sponsor side. Competing for Synergist/Wow/ALF money as a 20-person start-up is delusional.

## 22. 30-Day Validation Plan

Run **before** a website, a logo, or a “founding member” LinkedIn post.

### Days 1–10 — 25 owner interviews

Quota: 8 × 15–40 person independents; 5 × 5–14; 5 × 40–100; 4 PR vs 4 digital/performance vs 4 mixed; 3 already AH or TAC members.

Ask, in order:

1. Biggest problem this quarter (unprompted).
2. Who did you last tell, and did you show them numbers?
3. Which paid groups, coaches, accountants, forums in the last 24 months — and **what you paid**.
4. AH / TAC / Vistage / Helm / BenchPress: member? lapsed? why?
5. Would you sit with a non-overlapping competitor from this city?
6. Would you share utilisation, GP, debtor days, client % in a room of 8?
7. Six dates in 12 months: realistic or fantasy?
8. Company pay **£3,000** vs **£4,500** vs **£7,500** (with 4×1:1) — yes / only if X / no.
9. Who signs the PO?

### Days 1–10 — 10 supplier interviews

Accountants, agency-ops software, recruiters, PI insurance, M&A. Show: (a) logo, (b) nameless insight from 8, (c) hosted dinner Glenister-style. Ask for a number. Do not prompt £10k first.

### Days 11–20 — paid offer, not a “pilot night”

Written offer to 30 qualified names: **£750 for three meetings** (equivalent to £3,000/year), dates locked, NDA attached, **no refund after meeting 1**. Randomise 15 “AH-like community extras” vs 15 “silent board only”.

### Days 21–30 — one 3-hour board

8 people. Capture whether numbers were actually shared.

### Pass / fail

Logic: AH already converts this audience at £3k. You need evidence of **stolen or unserved demand**, not politeness.

1. Of 16 owners in the 15–40 band, **fewer than 6** name a problem they will not take to their accountant or AH Slack → no residual pain. **Stop.**
2. Of those 16, **fewer than 4** will expense **≥ £3,000** → cannot price a board. **Stop.**
3. Of 10 who have heard of Agency Hackers, **fewer than 3** say AH is “not for me because too broad / too festival / not owner-only” → no gap. **Stop.**
4. Of 20 receiving the paid offer, **fewer than 6 deposits** → acquisition will not work. **Stop.** (6/20 = 30% is already heroic against an incumbent.)
5. Pilot: **fewer than 6 of 8** put a real number on the table → it is networking. **Stop.**
6. Sponsors: if they will pay **≥ £8,000** only for names/dinner, you are being offered an access firm. Take it only if you **relabel**. Do not mix with the board.
7. If 4+ interviewees are happy AH members → you are recruiting their leftovers. **Stop.**

£3,000 is the AH public price, so it is the correct hurdle, not a vanity number.
