# Four Corners Strategic Research Report

**Independent market sizing, analogue analysis, and commercial challenge**

13 August 2026  
Prepared as a senior strategy assessment. This document is designed to challenge the working concept, not to validate it.

**Classification of estimates.** Figures marked **ONS/DBT** are official statistics. Figures marked **derived** are calculated from official statistics with stated assumptions. Figures marked **estimated** are judgemental and should not be treated as measured facts. Confidence is stated per section.

## 1. Executive Summary

Four Corners is exploring an independent organisation that helps HR, People, Learning and Organisational Development teams make better learning decisions. The working concept mixes buyer-side advice, a free managed learning service, a peer community, events, programme reviews, provider comparisons, buying guides and a resource library.

**The problem is real. The current product is not.** Large UK organisations do spend material money on external learning, and many L&D teams lack a rigorous way to choose leadership and organisational development partners. That is not the same as a market gap large enough, or empty enough, to support the full service list as a new independent business.

**The addressable buyer universe is small and already occupied.** There are **11,415** UK VAT/PAYE enterprises with 250 or more employees (ONS UK Business Counts / Nomis, March 2025). Of these, **9,390** are private-sector (including non-profits) and **2,020** are public-sector. Department for Business and Trade Business Population Estimates (BPE) 2025, which exclude government and not-for-profit, count **8,335** private large businesses. This is a concentrated market, not a mass market. London and the South East hold **37%** of large-enterprise head offices.

**The money that Four Corners can actually intercept is much smaller than “UK L&D spend”.** UK employers spent **£53.0 billion** on training in 2024, but **£26.2 billion** of that is trainee wages. Fees to external providers were **£3.8 billion** (7% of the total) (Department for Education, Employer Skills Survey 2024). A realistic economic TAM for external leadership, management and OD programmes among 250+ organisations is **£350–600 million**, not billions. A realistic service SAM for an independent advice / introduction / community business is **£15–40 million** of revenue potential. A realistic year-3 SOM is **£0.6–1.8 million**.

**The weakest assumption is “free to the client” combined with “independent”.** Every durable analogue that is free to the buyer is paid by the supplier (iVentiv, G2/Capterra, sponsored executive events). Every durable analogue that is genuinely independent is paid by the buyer (Gartner, Forrester, Fosway). Mixing the two at launch will destroy trust with the more sophisticated buyers the brand wants, and will attract the providers who most need distribution rather than the ones clients most need.

**If investing personal capital, do not launch the current portfolio.** Kill free managed learning, kill public programme reviews, and kill the resource library as a year-1 product. Launch a narrow, paid, buyer-funded peer council plus structured partner shortlisting for UK Heads of L&D in organisations with roughly 1,000–10,000 employees. That segment is too small for iVentiv’s 10,000-employee filter and too poorly served by Gartner’s price point.

**Highest-priority commercial direction**

1. Choose one economic engine before any brand work: **buyer-pays independence**.
2. Beachhead: **private and public organisations with 1,000–9,999 employees**, UK HQ, with a named Head of L&D / Leadership / OD.
3. Offer: 12-person peer councils, a documented shortlisting method, and post-implementation “lessons learned” interviews under Chatham House rules.
4. Price: **£8,000–£15,000** per organisation per year. No supplier payment in year 1.
5. Prove 25 paying organisations and 40 documented case interviews before adding events, reviews or a marketplace.

## 2. UK Market Analysis

### 2.1 What “the market” actually is

The brief asks for organisations with approximately 250+ employees that are likely to have dedicated L&D, Talent, OD or People Development functions. Three counting conventions are in use in UK official statistics. Using the wrong one will mis-size the business by 20–30%.

| Source | What it counts | 250+ count | Use for Four Corners |
|---|---|---|---|
| DBT Business Population Estimates 2025 (start of 2025) | Private-sector businesses only. Excludes government and not-for-profit. Includes an estimate of unregistered businesses (irrelevant at 250+). | **8,335** | Private-sector commercial buyers |
| ONS / Nomis UK Business Counts, March 2025 | VAT and/or PAYE registered **enterprises**, all legal statuses, head office location | **11,415** | Best single account universe, including public sector and charities |
| ONS UK Business: activity, size and location 2025 | Same IDBR extract as Nomis. 2.73 million registered businesses in total | Same register | Cross-check |

**Recommendation:** use **11,415 enterprises** as the organisational TAM. BPE’s 8,335 figure understates the buyer set because NHS trusts, local authorities, universities, police forces, large charities and housing associations are exactly the organisations that employ Heads of L&D and buy leadership programmes.

**Enterprise vs local unit.** A supermarket group is one enterprise and hundreds of local units. L&D buying almost always sits at enterprise (or group) level. Do not use local-unit counts. Do not use establishment counts from the Employer Skills Survey as if they were company counts. ESS is a survey of **sites**, not enterprises. That distinction matters for spend, and is flagged wherever ESS is used below.

The commercial implication is stable either way: the true “enterprise L&D” core is a few thousand organisations, not tens of thousands.

### 2.4 Public vs private

| Segment | 250+ enterprises | Share | Commercial note |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Private companies | 8,235 | 72% | Fastest path to a discretionary leadership-programme budget |
| Non-profit / mutual | 1,105 | 10% | Real buyers; lower fees; high values-fit if independence is genuine |

BPE’s 8,335 private large businesses is the right figure if Four Corners explicitly excludes public and charity. That would be a mistake. Public and non-profit together are **~3,125** organisations, many with dedicated OD functions and recurring leadership programmes. They are also the buyers most allergic to undisclosed supplier commission.

**Public-sector procurement is a substitute, not an adjacent channel.** Crown Commercial Service frameworks, NHS procurement hubs and local-authority frameworks already perform the “safe shortlist” function that Four Corners wants to sell. A new independent advisor that is not on those frameworks will struggle to be used for contract award, even if L&D likes the advice. This is a structural constraint, not a sales-skill problem.

### 2.6 Typical internal structure of L&D teams

Evidence is thinner than the organisation counts. The best UK sources are CIPD surveys, which are practitioner samples, not census data. Treat team-size figures as directional.

**Where L&D sits (CIPD, Learning and Skills at Work 2020)**

- In **59%** of organisations, L&D is inside or attached to HR.
- In **41%**, L&D is structurally separate: different reporting line (11%), inside a business function (10%), or owned by operations/line managers (18%).
- Separation from HR is much more common in smaller organisations. In large organisations, a named L&D function is the norm.

**Team size (CIPD 2020, organisations that have an L&D function)**

| Organisation size | Median L&D headcount | Mean L&D headcount |
|---|---:|---:|
| 250–999 | 4 | 24 |
| 1,000+ | 12 | 166 |
| All 250+ | 8 | 118 |
| SME (<250) | 1 | 7 |

Source: CIPD (2020), Table 2. Base: 972 respondents with an L&D function. The mean is pulled up by a small number of very large academies (banks, grocers, civil service, NHS). The **median is the planning number**.

CIPD’s older Learning and Development 2015 survey, still widely cited for banded medians, showed: 250–999 median 3; 1,000–4,999 median 6; 5,000–19,999 median 18; 20,000+ median 53. Directionally consistent with 2020.

**Roles that actually exist (CIPD 2020)**

Most common in organisations with an L&D function: administrator, Head of L&D / L&D manager, face-to-face trainer. Then L&D business partner, online facilitator, coaching lead. “Chief Learning Officer” remains rare in the UK outside global corporates and a handful of professional-services firms.

**CIPD Learning at Work 2023 (1,108 UK-heavy practitioners)**

- 53% of those in L&D functions reported increased workload.
- About a quarter reported increased budget and headcount.
- The operating reality is more work, not more buying capacity.

**So what.** In the 250–499 band, “dedicated L&D” often means one generalist plus borrowed HR. They do not have time to join a peer community, attend quarterly events, and run a managed learning service RFP. Capacity appears in a durable way from **~1,000 employees** upward, and becomes a true make-vs-buy function from **~5,000**.

### 2.7 Likely decision makers

| Decision | Typical owner in 250–999 | Typical owner in 1,000–4,999 | Typical owner in 5,000+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identify leadership need | HRD, senior directors, line (CIPD: L&D owns needs in 27% of large orgs vs 5% of SMEs) | Head of L&D / Leadership; HRBP | CLO / Group Head of L&D; business-unit academy |
| Own the learning budget | HRD or finance; L&D owns budget in **31%** of large orgs (CIPD 2020) | Head of L&D with HRD approval | CLO with investment committee; procurement for large contracts |
| Select a provider | Head of L&D + HRD | Head of L&D + procurement | Procurement-led with L&D scoring; sometimes Gartner/Fosway input |
| Sign the contract | HRD / FD | HRD / procurement | Procurement / commercial legal |
| Kill or renew a programme | HRD / ExCo sponsor | ExCo sponsor + L&D | Business-unit P&L owner + CLO |

**The economic buyer is rarely the L&D practitioner Four Corners will meet first.** CIPD data is explicit: even in large organisations, L&D owns the budget in a minority of cases. Senior directors and line managers still identify a large share of needs. A product that only serves the Head of L&D, without a procurement- and HRD-safe artefact (shortlist, comparison, risk note), will generate interest and lose at award.

Public sector adds standing commercial and framework owners. NHS and local government L&D cannot “just pick” a leadership provider if the value exceeds threshold.

**Derived estimate of external fees inside 250+ enterprises: £1.1–1.6 billion.** Method: 7% fee share applied to large-organisation training. Private large employment is 11.25 million (BPE). **Leadership / management / OD slice (estimated): £350–600 million.** Method: ESS reports 34% of *training employers* provided management training in 2024 (up from 32% in 2022), and 76% of 100+ sites provided off-the-job training. Applied to £1.1–1.6 billion, that is **£220–560 million**, rounded to **£350–600 million** to include executive coaching and custom OD that buyers sometimes code outside “training”. Confidence: **low-to-medium**. This is the number that matters, and it is the one no official source publishes.

Four Corners is entering a shrinking spend pool.

### 2.9 Organisations that regularly buy leadership, management or OD programmes

No official series exists. The estimate below is a participation model, not a measurement.

**Assumptions**

- 1,000+ enterprises: 85–95% buy some form of management or leadership development in a two-year window (near-universal among organisations with a named L&D function).
- 500–999: 55–70%.
- 250–499: 30–45% (many rely on ILM/CMI open programmes, apprenticeships, or ad hoc coaches).
- “Regularly” = at least one externally provided leadership/management/OD programme per year, not including statutory management apprenticeships only.

| Size | Enterprises | Regular buyers (central) | Range |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 250–499 | 5,485 | 2,050 | 1,650–2,470 |
| 500–999 | 2,900 | 1,810 | 1,600–2,030 |
| 1,000+ | 3,030 | 2,730 | 2,575–2,880 |
| **Total** | **11,415** | **~6,600** | **~5,800–7,400** |

Confidence: **low**. Apprenticeship Levy distorts the picture: some “leadership programmes” are levy-funded management apprenticeships bought from FE and specialist providers, which is a different buying motion (ESFA rules, 20% off-the-job, procurement via apprenticeship service) than a custom OD intervention.

**So what.** The live buyer set is about **6,000–7,000** organisations, not 11,400. The set that will pay for help choosing a provider is a fraction of that: organisations running a **custom or retained** programme, not an ILM Level 3 off-the-shelf course. That is the SAM, and it is likely **2,000–3,500** organisations.

### 2.10 TAM, SAM, SOM

Definitions used here are specific to Four Corners, not textbook decoration.

**TAM — Total Addressable Market**

Two layers:

1. **Account TAM:** **11,400** UK organisations with 250+ employees (Nomis, March 2025). This is who *could* theoretically use an independent learning-decision service.
2. **Economic TAM:** **£350–600 million** per year of external leadership, management, coaching and OD programme spend inside that account set (estimated from ESS external fees). If Four Corners were a training provider, this would be its market. It is not. If Four Corners is an advisor/introducer, it can only intercept a **take rate** on this spend, or a **membership fee** unrelated to spend.

**SAM — Serviceable Available Market**

Filters applied:

- Has a named L&D / Leadership / OD / Talent Development leader (rare below 500; common above 1,000).
- Buys custom or retained leadership/OD, not only catalogue compliance training.
- UK-headquartered or UK-autonomous (global academies in US/EU HQs are not realistically won from a new UK brand).
- Willing to use an intermediary (excludes organisations locked to a single MLS outsourcer, a single business school, or a CCS framework with no room for advice).

**Account SAM: 2,000–3,500 organisations**, centred on 500–9,999 employees in private, public and large non-profit, with a dedicated learning leader.

**Revenue SAM, two mutually exclusive models:**

| Model | Mechanic | Revenue SAM | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Buyer-pays | £8k–£15k membership × 2,000–3,500 | **£16–53 million** | Cleaner; harder to sell; matches Fosway/Gartner logic at a lower price |
| B. Supplier-pays | 8–12% introduction fee on £250–400 million of interceptable SAM spend | **£20–48 million** | Larger headline; independence problem; matches iVentiv/G2 logic |

Do not add A and B. They fight each other.

**SOM — Serviceable Obtainable Market (year 3, execution-realistic)**

New B2B trust businesses in a relationship market do not take 5% of SAM in three years. Comparables:

- Fosway is a small specialist analyst firm after 20+ years, not a £50 million business.
- iVentiv has run 300+ events over 18 years for a thin slice of global 10,000+ employee companies.
- LPI and similar bodies remain modest membership organisations.

**Year-3 SOM (recommended Model A):** 80–150 member organisations × £10k average = **£0.8–1.5 million**, plus £0–0.3 million from one annual flagship event. Total **£0.8–1.8 million**.

**Year-3 SOM (if Model B is chosen instead):** 30–50 paying providers × £20k–£40k + events = **£0.9–2.2 million**, with higher churn and a permanent independence discount.

**Five-year ceiling without a platform or software product:** **£4–8 million** revenue, and only if the beachhead is held with high retention. That is a specialist advisory/community firm, not a category-defining marketplace.

## 3. Competitor Review

The relevant competitors are not training companies and not CIPD-as-membership-body. They are organisations that combine some of: independent buying advice, community, peer networking, case evidence, events, provider introductions, research, libraries, procurement support.

They fall into five economic families. Four Corners is trying to live in several families at once. That is the strategic error.

### 3.1 Map of analogue families

| Family | Who pays | What the buyer gets | Closest names | Fit to Four Corners |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Buyer-funded analyst / advisor | Client organisation | Research, inquiry, shortlists, cover-your-back | Gartner, Forrester, Fosway, Josh Bersin Company, Brandon Hall, RedThread | Strongest *trust* analogue |
| 2. Supplier-funded buyer community / events | Vendors, sponsors | Free or cheap access, peer rooms, 1:1s with vendors | iVentiv, G2, Capterra (now G2), many “executive exchanges” | Strongest *go-to-market* analogue for “free MLS” |
| 3. Managed learning outsourcers | Client (large SOW) or mixed | Run the learning function / vendor panel | GP Strategies (LTG), NIIT, Infopro, Hemsley Fraser MLS, Reed Learning | Direct threat if Four Corners offers MLS |
| 4. Expert networks | Client (per call or seat) | Paid conversations with practitioners | GLG, AlphaSights, Third Bridge | Analogue for “lessons learned interviews” |
| 5. Closed peer councils | Member (often company-paid) | Small trusted rooms, no vendors in the room | Chief, World50 / CMO/CHRO councils, EAB/Advisory Board heritage | Analogue for the community people actually value |

Traditional conferences (Learning Technologies as a trade show), training providers, and generic HR memberships are out of scope except where they substitute for a Four Corners service.

### 3.2 Profiles

#### Fosway Group (UK / Europe) — closest specialist analogue

- **Business model:** Independent HR, talent and learning technology analyst. Corporate engagement contracts, project support, and (since 2024) individual membership of the Fosway Corporate Network, including a no-cost Associate tier.
- **Revenue model:** Buyer-pays (corporate contracts, membership, service credits). Separate vendor programme: vendors pay for insight and exposure, which is the standard analyst dual-revenue pattern. 9-Grid research is the magnet.
- **Target:** European and global corporate HR/L&D leaders buying **systems**, not classroom programmes.
- **Services:** 9-Grid comparisons, strategy validation, vendor due diligence, roundtables, analyst inquiry.
- **Pricing:** Not published. Directionally well below Gartner; still a budget line, not a free utility.
- **Strengths:** 20+ years of European data; genuine independence claim; already the default “who should we shortlist?” for LMS/LXP/HR tech in EMEA; roundtables of 250+ leaders.
- **Weaknesses:** Tech-centric. Weak coverage of leadership-programme *content* providers (business schools, consultancies, coach networks). Small firm. Associate-free tier risks training buyers not to pay.
- **Differentiation:** The only widely used independent vendor-comparison tool designed for European HR/learning tech.
- **Innovative?** Incrementally. 9-Grid is a Gartner MQ alternative, not a new category.
- **Lesson:** Independence is a product. Fosway can talk to vendors because corporates pay for the advice. Copy the **corporate engagement + credits** mechanic. Do not copy the free Associate tier if you need revenue.

#### Gartner (global) — the expensive cover-your-back standard

- **Business model:** Research subscription + inquiry + conferences + (historically) Digital Markets review sites.
- **Revenue model:** Buyer-pays seats. Vendr and similar buyer-reported ranges: roughly **$25,000–$150,000** per organisation per year for research, **$100,000–$500,000+** for broader enterprise packages. G-Cloud list price example: Gartner for CHROs Executive **£24,100 per licence per year**. Advisory hours often **$3,000–$7,000**.
- **Target:** Fortune/FTSE and public-sector enterprises. HR is one practice among many.
- **Services:** Research, Magic Quadrant / Hype Cycle, Peer Insights, Peer Connect, conferences (ReimagineHR), CHRO advisory.
- **Strengths:** Procurement already accepts Gartner as a defensible source. Peer Insights gives software review scale. Deep sales coverage.
- **Weaknesses:** Poor on boutique UK leadership providers. Expensive. Inquiry quality varies. Dual relationship with vendors is a known tension. In January–February 2026 Gartner **sold Capterra, Software Advice and GetApp to G2** (~$110 million per Gartner 10-K commentary), concentrating the review-marketplace model outside Gartner.
- **Innovative?** Historically yes; now an incumbent.
- **Lesson:** Buyers will pay high five to six figures when the output is **politically useful** (a document they can take to the CIO/CFO). Four Corners needs a Gartner-like artefact — a shortlist memo — not a newsletter.

#### Forrester (global)

- Similar family to Gartner, typically 10–25% cheaper on comparable seats (buyer-reported). Waves more often sold à la carte (historically ~$2,500–$4,000). Stronger on customer-experience and some tech; not a UK leadership-programme advisor. Lesson: a single-report purchase path is easier to trial than a membership.

#### The Josh Bersin Company (global)

- **Model:** Corporate membership (research, Big Reset senior network, Irresistible conference, Galileo AI assistant) plus **vendor membership**.
- **Revenue:** Dual. Corporates pay for research/community/AI; vendors pay for access and positioning.
- **Target:** Global HR executives, not UK mid-market L&D.
- **Strengths:** Brand, AI packaging (Galileo), convening of CHRO-level people.
- **Weaknesses:** US-centric; vendor membership is visible; expensive relative to UK 1,000-employee HRD budgets; more “future of work” than provider due diligence.
- **Innovative?** Galileo is one of the few genuinely new analyst products in this space.
- **Lesson:** If you cannot staff 20 analysts, do not imitate the research factory. Imitate the **small senior network** (Big Reset), which is closer to what Four Corners can actually operate.

#### iVentiv (UK-founded, global) — closest “free to buyer” analogue in L&D

- **Model:** Executive Knowledge Exchanges. **Free for VP/Director-level leaders from companies with 10,000+ employees.** Max ~40 participants. 1:1 meetings with event partners (vendors) during breaks. 18 years, 300+ events, claimed 93% satisfaction.
- **Revenue:** Sponsorship / event partner fees. Travel not included.
- **Target:** Group-level learning and talent leaders in very large corporates.
- **Strengths:** Clear filter (10,000+). Buyer-side experience feels premium. Longevity. Word-of-mouth.
- **Weaknesses:** Explicit vendor 1:1s. Useless to the 250–9,999 employee majority. Not advice, not a shortlist, not MLS. Invitation politics.
- **Innovative?** The format is a polished version of a sponsored roundtable. Execution quality is the innovation.
- **Lesson:** “Free for the right buyer, paid by suppliers, tightly gated” works. iVentiv has already taken the global 10,000+ learning-leader event. Four Corners should not fight for those 200–400 UK individuals. It should own the **1,000–9,999** band iVentiv rejects.

#### G2 (and Capterra / Software Advice / GetApp) — the review marketplace

- **Model:** Free for software buyers. Vendors pay for profiles, badges, intent data, ads. G2 acquired Gartner’s Digital Markets trio in early 2026, combining ~6 million reviews and a claimed 200 million annual buyer reach.
- **Vendor pricing (buyer-reported, 2026):** G2 Starter ~$2.7–3.0k; Professional ~$13.5–17.7k; Enterprise ~$21–28k, much higher with add-ons.
- **Strengths:** Scale, SEO, intent data, a standard that procurement recognises for **software**.
- **Weaknesses:** **Does not travel to bespoke services.** A three-day leadership journey for a bank is not an LMS SKU. Fake-review and pay-to-play accusations are a permanent tax. Sample size for niche providers is poor.
- **Innovative?** Category-creating in software; a trap if copied onto programmes.
- **Lesson:** Online Programme Reviews will not become “G2 for leadership training”. The unit of analysis is wrong. If Four Corners wants reviews, make them **structured case interviews**, not star ratings.

#### GP Strategies / LTG, NIIT, Infopro, Hemsley Fraser — managed learning services

- **Model:** Multi-year outsourcing of learning operations, vendor management, content, delivery. Buyer pays (often millions per year). NelsonHall (2022) estimated the global learning services market at ~$3.9 billion (2021) growing to ~$6.9 billion (2026), UK growth among the faster regions.
- **Strengths:** Entrenched in global 5,000+ employers. They already “manage the panel” and will treat a free MLS as amateur hour.
- **Weaknesses:** Heavy; slow; perceived as operational not strategic; independence of vendor choice is limited by their own delivery P&L.
- **Lesson:** “Free MLS” is not a clever wedge. It is an under-funded version of a market that already charges. Either price MLS properly later, or stay out.

#### GLG, AlphaSights, Third Bridge — expert networks

- **Model:** Clients pay per call or via seats to speak to practitioners. Experts are paid. Compliance-heavy.
- **Lesson for “Lessons Learned interviews”:** The product people will pay for is **access to someone who has just implemented what I am about to buy**, with legal safe harbour. Four Corners can do a slower, cheaper, higher-trust version inside a members’ council. It cannot out-GLG GLG.

#### EAB / The Advisory Board Company (heritage) — the gold-standard research + peer + events firm

- **Model (historic Advisory Board, healthcare; EAB in education):** Membership research organisation. Hospitals/universities paid substantial annual fees for best-practice studies, peer meetings, and implementation support. Vendors were not the customer.
- **Why it mattered:** It sold **reduction of executive anxiety** in complex, reputationally dangerous decisions.
- **Lesson:** This is the best analogue Four Corners could choose — and the hardest to fund. It requires a research engine and 10+ years. Do not claim this position in year 1. Borrow the **membership + original inquiry + peer meeting** spine.

#### Chief (US, expanding)

- **Model:** Paid executive community for women VP+. Packages from **$5,900** (education/advisory) and **$7,500** (coaching) per year; often company-sponsored.
- **Lesson:** People pay several thousand pounds for a **facilitated small room**, not for a Slack group and a resource library. Price to the room.

#### Brandon Hall Group; RedThread Research; Training Industry; LPI

- Brandon Hall: research + awards + Institute membership (publicly cited around $99/month for an individual plan; enterprise is custom). Awards are a vendor-revenue engine dressed as excellence.
- RedThread: small, sharp HR research; team memberships from the high hundreds of dollars — too small to be a threat, useful as a content partner.
- Training Industry: US media + “Top 20” lists; vendor-funded; **not independent advice**.
- LPI: professional body for L&D practitioners; community and CPD; not a buying advisor. Competes for **attention and event diaries**, not for shortlisting fees.

#### Other substitutes (not “competitors” but they steal the job)

- **LinkedIn:** informal peer advice. Free, noisy, already the default.
- **CIPD:** research, branches, magazine. Not a buying service; still the UK L&D identity brand.
- **Learning Technologies / UNLEASH:** vendor showcases. Good enough for many 250–999 buyers.
- **CCS / NHS frameworks:** the public-sector shortlist.
- **Incumbent providers and “safe” business schools** (Ashridge/Hult, Henley, Oxford Saïd, Cambridge Judge, LBS, Roffey Park, etc.): switching costs are social, not informational.

### 3.3 Who is actually innovative

| Organisation | Innovative? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| G2 (especially post-Capterra acquisition) | Yes, in software discovery | Scale + intent + now category consolidation |
| Josh Bersin / Galileo | Yes | Packaging research as an HR copilot |
| iVentiv | No, but excellent craft | Tight gating + sponsored intimacy |
| Fosway | No | Necessary specialist; evolutionary |
| Gartner | No longer | Incumbent bureaucracy with a still-powerful sales machine |
| Advisory Board / EAB (heritage) | Yes, historically | Invented paid research membership for operators |
| Expert networks | Yes, 2000s innovation, now mature | Monetised practitioner knowledge |
| “Free MLS + independent advice” | Unproven | No high-quality analogue successfully holds both claims |

**There is no successful, scaled “independent G2 for leadership programmes”.** That absence is not proof of a gap. It is more likely proof that **bespoke services do not support a review-marketplace**.

### 3.4 Implications for Four Corners

The working concept is a collage of Fosway (advice), iVentiv (events), G2 (reviews), CIPD/LPI (community and library), GP Strategies (MLS), and GLG (lessons learned). Each of those firms chose **one** economic engine. Four Corners has not.

## 4. Strategic Insights

### 4.1 Does this business solve a meaningful problem?

**Yes, a narrow one.** The meaningful problem is not “I cannot find a leadership provider”. Google, LinkedIn and incumbent relationships solve discovery. The problem is:

> “I am about to spend £150,000–£1 million on a leadership or OD intervention. I will be personally blamed if it is theatre. I do not have a defensible way to choose, and I cannot talk honestly to peers who have just done it.”

That problem is acute in **1,000–10,000 employee** organisations: big enough to spend, small enough to lack a CLO office, Gartner seat, and MLS outsourcer.

It is **not** acute in 250–499 organisations in the way the concept assumes. Their constraint is time and budget, not independent market maps.

It is **not** acute in 20,000+ globals in the way a new UK brand can capture. They already have Fosway, Gartner, iVentiv, and often an MLS.

### 4.2 Strongest assumptions

1. **250+ is the right *lower* threshold for a dedicated function — for planning, not for sales.** CIPD medians support “some L&D capacity” at 250+. Sales capacity starts higher.
2. **Leadership and OD remain externally purchased.** ESS shows management training incidence rising slightly (34% of training employers) even as total spend falls. Custom leadership work has not been fully in-housed.
3. **Peers beat content.** Every analogue that retains members (Chief, iVentiv, Fosway roundtables, Advisory Board) sells other operators, not PDFs.
4. **Independence is a buying criterion for a subset of HRDs**, especially public sector, regulated financial services, and anyone who has been burned by a sponsored “recommendation”.

### 4.3 Weakest assumptions

1. **“Free MLS to clients” can be independent.** It cannot, except as a loss-leader funded by hidden supplier margin. Mortgage-broker and comparison-site history is unambiguous: the paying side shapes the funnel. Sophisticated HRDs know this.
2. **The bottleneck is finding providers.** For most 250+ organisations the bottleneck is internal: sponsor quality, time off the job, cynical middle managers, inability to measure, and procurement friction. A better catalogue does not fix that.
3. **Online programme reviews will work like software reviews.** Programmes are co-produced, confidential, and small-n. Defamation risk is real. Providers will not submit to a star rating they cannot control. Buyers will not write reviews that embarrass their ExCo.
4. **A resource library is an asset.** CIPD, LPI, Fosway, LinkedIn, and a hundred consultancies already oversupply content. Libraries do not convert to willingness to pay.
5. **Kitchen-sink launch.** Events + community + MLS + reviews + guides + interviews is a five-year product roadmap pretending to be a year-1 company.
6. **Public sector will behave like private.** Frameworks, standstill periods, and audit will dummy-out informal introductions.
7. **Geographic brand (“Four Corners”) matches the buyer map.** It does not. 37% of large HQs are in London and the South East; 89% are in England.

### 4.4 What the spend data actually says

Training spend is falling. External **fees** are a thin slice (£3.8 billion nationally; perhaps £1.1–1.6 billion inside 250+ organisations; perhaps £350–600 million in leadership/OD). Four Corners does not get that £350–600 million. It gets a membership fee or a take rate. Plan as a **£1–2 million** business in year 3, or do not start.

### 4.5 Unit economics that should scare a founder

| Activity | Cost reality | Who has made it work |
|---|---|---|
| Quality peer event (40 people, facilitated) | £15k–£40k cash plus 4–8 weeks of BD | iVentiv, via sponsors |
| Original “lessons learned” interview, write-up, legal review | £1.5k–£4k fully loaded | Expert networks, at high prices |
| Maintained provider comparison | Analyst time every quarter; vendor relations | Fosway, Gartner |
| MLS operations | Staff, systems, SLAs, insurance | GP Strategies, NIIT |
| Community management | A full-time human, or it dies | Chief (at $6k–7.5k/member) |

A free-to-client model must therefore either (a) raise prices on suppliers high enough to fund this, which recreates iVentiv, or (b) under-deliver, which recreates a LinkedIn group.

## 5. Risks

| Risk | Why it is material | Early warning |
|---|---|---|
| **Independence collapse** | Supplier-pay + “independent recommendations” is the fastest way to lose the only brand asset | First 10 recommended providers all happen to be sponsors |
| **Cold start** | Communities without a paid, facilitated core never reach density. L&D people already have CIPD, LinkedIn, and provider clubs | Event attendance <25 qualified buyers; WhatsApp group goes quiet |
| **Wrong segment** | 250–499 looks big (5,485) and buys little advice | Lots of “interested” SMEs, no PO |
| **Incumbent squeeze** | Fosway (tech), iVentiv (events for giants), MLS majors (operations), Gartner (enterprise cover) | “We already have Fosway / we’re on a framework” |
| **Procurement veto** | Especially NHS, local government, universities | L&D loves it; commercial says no |
| **Defamation / programme reviews** | Bespoke programmes + public scores | First angry letter from a provider’s lawyer |
| **Confidentiality of lessons learned** | Firms will share success theatre, not failure | Case studies that read like vendor collateral |
| **Spend contraction** | ESS: lowest real training spend since 2011 | Membership sold as “nice to have” and cut in budget rounds |
| **Key-person** | Trust businesses are the founder’s Rolodex until year 4 | Pipeline is 80% one relationship |
| **Adverse selection of providers** | The best leadership faculties (top schools, elite consultancies) do not pay for introductions | Marketplace fills with hungry mid-tier firms |
| **Levy distortion** | Management apprenticeships look like “leadership programmes” and follow ESFA rules | Time wasted on a buying motion Four Corners cannot influence |
| **Brand geography** | “Four Corners” vs London HQ reality | Regional events with thin rooms |

## 6. Opportunities (including those not in the working concept)

1. **Own the 1,000–9,999 UK band.** iVentiv’s 10,000+ cut leaves thousands of Heads of L&D without a serious peer room. This is the single best white space in the research.
2. **Sell a Gartner-like artefact at a Fosway-like price:** a 12-page “decision record” for a specific leadership-programme buy (criteria, five firms, trade-offs, references). Price £6k–£12k per decision, or include in membership.
3. **Chatham House implementation reviews**, not public star ratings. GLG-like value, membership-like packaging.
4. **Public-sector track as a separate product**, built around frameworks rather than pretending frameworks do not exist: “which framework suppliers actually deliver leadership, and what did the last three trusts learn?”
5. **Procurement joint-venture.** Partner with a public-sector procurement specialist rather than becoming one.
6. **Provider quality system without a marketplace:** accreditation of *process* (evaluation design, ethical contracting, faculty transparency) rather than ranking of brands.
7. **HRD, not just Head of L&D, as the economic buyer.** CIPD: L&D owns the budget in only 31% of large organisations. The product must be forwardable to an HRD.
8. **Unloved sectors:** large housing associations, multi-academy trusts, and charities with 250–2,000 staff. Low Gartner penetration, real OD problems, values-aligned if independence is real.
9. **Do not build software in year 1.** The failed pattern in this space is a platform with no density. A Notion plus a facilitator beats an empty app.
10. **Later, not now:** a paid MLS for 1,000–4,999 organisations that have outgrown a coordinator and cannot afford GP Strategies. That is a different company.

## 7. Recommended Direction

### 7.1 If investing my own money, what would change before launch

1. **Kill “free MLS”.** It collides with independence and with firms that already do MLS. Revisit in year 4 as a priced operational product, or never.
2. **Kill public Online Programme Reviews.** Replace with private, sampled, interviewer-mediated case files.
3. **Kill the resource library as a product.** Link to CIPD and move on.
4. **Park quarterly open events** until 40 members exist. Events without density are expensive mixers for providers.
5. **Choose Model A: buyer pays.** Publish a one-page independence charter: no referral fees, no pay-to-play shortlists, supplier conversations only as research.
6. **Narrow the beachhead** to UK organisations with 1,000–9,999 employees and a named Head of L&D / Leadership / OD. Include NHS trusts, councils, universities and large charities in that size band as a second wave, not as a blur.
7. **Product = three things only for 18 months:**
   - Facilitated peer council (10–12 Heads of L&D, four meetings a year).
   - Decision-record shortlisting (one live buy per member per year).
   - Lessons-learned interview archive (target 40 in year 1), Chatham House, named sector/size, anonymised organisation unless permission is written.
8. **Price £8,000–£15,000 per organisation per year**, company-paid, 12-month term. Founder-led delivery. No app.
9. **Do not hire a content team.** Hire (or contract) one facilitator-researcher and one operator.
10. **Rename or subtitle for search and honesty.** “Four Corners” does not say what it is. The market needs “independent leadership-programme decisions for UK learning leaders”, however that is branded.

### 7.2 What to build, what to kill

| Working idea | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Independent partner recommendations | **Build, as a paid decision record** | This is the job to be done |
| Managed Learning Service, free to clients | **Kill** | Conflicts with independence; under-gunned vs MLS majors |
| Peer learning community | **Build, as paid councils of 12, not an open community** | Open communities lose to LinkedIn |
| Lessons Learned interviews | **Build, private archive** | Highest unique-asset potential |
| Quarterly events | **Defer** | Until member density exists |
| Online Programme Reviews | **Kill** (public) | Unit of analysis is wrong; legal and quality risk |
| Provider comparisons | **Build, as living shortlists inside membership** | Not as a public G2 clone |
| Buying guides | **Thin, not a pillar** | Commodity content |
| Resource library | **Kill as a product** | Oversupplied |

### 7.3 Beachhead

**Primary:** Private UK enterprises, 1,000–4,999 employees, professional services, financial services, knowledge manufacturing, technology, and specialist engineering. Head of L&D reports to HRD. Budget £150k–£500k external leadership/OD.

**Secondary (months 9–18):** NHS trusts and upper-tier local authorities in the same size spirit, with a framework-aware version of the decision record.

**Do not prioritise:** 250–499 (capacity); 20,000+ globals (incumbents); open-enrolment ILM/CMI catalogue buyers (no advice need).

### 7.4 Why this is still a hard business

Even the narrowed version is a **trust firm with a few thousand possible customers and a falling spend pool**. It can be a good specialist living, a respected brand, and a platform for later products. It is unlikely to be a venture-scale marketplace. Anyone pitching it as “the Gartner of L&D” should be asked which Gartner cost base they intend to fund.

## 8. Five highest-priority next actions

1. **Run 25 structured discovery interviews in 30 days** with Heads of L&D / HRDs in UK organisations of 1,000–9,999 employees. Do not pitch. Measure: last leadership-programme buy, who signed, whether they used any advisor, what failed, willingness to pay £10k for a peer council plus one decision record. Kill or proceed on that evidence, not on this desk report.

2. **Write and legally review an independence charter and a Chatham House case-interview protocol** before any supplier conversation. If you are not willing to forgo referral fees in year 1, stop using the word independent.

3. **Build the beachhead list of 400 named organisations and 400 named humans** from the 1,000–4,999 private band plus 100 public/non-profit. Source: LinkedIn + Companies House + NHS provider list. This replaces “11,000 organisations” as the operating TAM.

4. **Sell 12 founding members at £8k** (or three councils of 12 at a founder discount) before building a website beyond a one-pager. Delivery is the product. No events, no app, no library.

5. **Commission a paid Orbis/Fame or ONS ad-hoc extract** for (a) 5,000+ employee enterprise counts and (b) 250+ counts by SIC section. This is a one-week data gap, not a strategy gap — but it will stop the team arguing about whether the market is 8,000 or 12,000.
