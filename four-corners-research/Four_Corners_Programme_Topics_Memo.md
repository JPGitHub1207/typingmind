**WORKING CONCLUSION**

**Do not run two loosely connected talks.** That is a mini-conference. The tables cannot interrogate two problems, the harvest of Corners goes scatter, and the letter cannot name what the afternoon is *for*.

**Do not copy iVentiv’s empty agenda into a 3.5-hour paid ticket.** iVentiv’s looseness is real, and it is earned: two days, max 40, pre-event Pulse survey, peer facilitators, free for a CLO who already knows the format. **Verified**, iventiv.com event pages and “Designing Events for Knowledge Sharing.” You have 3.5 hours and a £400 invoice. Procurement needs a sentence. “We’ll see what the room wants” is not that sentence.

**Steal iVentiv’s good bit, which you already designed:** the room names what continues. That is the Corners harvest. Keep the talks as a **shared problem with two angles**, not two guest spots.

**First paid date spine (recommended):** one problem — **why leadership programmes still don’t change managers** — two 20-minute provocations that both serve that problem. AI, skills, culture, and measurement show up as *lenses*, not as a second afternoon bolted on.

The canceled 25 August lineup (wellbeing / 70:20:10 / skills-based OD) is the anti-pattern: three famous topics, no shared job. Do not rebuild it paid.

## 1. Two loosely connected talks — review

The founder design is: two talks of **20 minutes**, table interrogation after each, then harvest four Corners. That spine is tight and right. The risk is **what the two talks are**.

| Pairing | What the room can do | What you can sell | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. One problem, two angles** (e.g. “the programme” and “the system around the manager”) | One interrogation, Corners are variations of the same job | One sentence on the letter | **Do this** |
| **B. Two loosely connected topics** (e.g. wellbeing + skills-based org) | Two shallow conversations, harvest is a shopping list | “Interesting speakers” | **Don’t.** Mini-festival |
| **C. Two unconnected celebrity talks** | Applause, then canapés | Names, not a room | **Don’t.** You already rejected bureau keynotes |
| **D. Fully open like iVentiv** (no named problem in advance) | Works over two days with a pre-survey | Hard to expense at £400 | **Don’t, for 3.5 hours** |

**Why B fails in this specific machine**

- 70 minutes of table time, split across two topics, is **35 minutes each**. That is not enough to get past “we’re all struggling with X.”
- Four Corners harvested from two unrelated talks will be four different products. You already said: run the **one or two** that fill, not all four QR codes. Unrelated talks make it more likely you get four weak groups instead of one strong one.
- The Head of L&D in a 400-person housing association did not come to hear a GSK-scale skills taxonomy *and* a TEDx wellbeing story in the same afternoon. They came to leave with a decision they can take back on Monday.
- Marketing: Wave A letters need one URL and one reason. “Two excellent contributors on loosely related themes” is the 25 August page.

**The 20-minute clock still holds.** If a speaker needs 40 minutes because the topics are different, you have already lost the tables. Two short talks only work if they are **in argument with each other**, not merely adjacent.

**If the two people you have booked cannot share a question, one of them is wrong for this date.** Chemistry is not a programme.

## 2. Should it be looser, like iVentiv?

### What iVentiv actually does (not the slogan)

Public claims, **verified** on 2026 event pages and iVentiv’s own knowledge-sharing write-up:

- Max **40**. “Every session is led by you.” “No sit and listen sessions.”
- Topics “identified by you and your peers.”
- **Pre-event surveys and Pulse Reports** — the agenda is co-created *before* people arrive, not invented in the first coffee.
- Facilitators are often **peers in the room** (Heads of L&D at Jet2, Virgin Media O2, LSEG, WPP Media, Expedia, etc. listed on Learning Futures London, 17–18 March 2026).
- Two days. London 2026 examples: March (Bloomsbury Hotel) and November (Lloyds Banking Group offices).
- **Free** for approved senior leaders at multinational companies. Fill method is sponsors / 1-2-1s, not a ticket. Already covered in the event-machine memo.

So “looser like iVentiv” is **not** “turn up and see.” It is: survey → named tracks the cohort already voted for → facilitated breakouts → a Pulse panel. The looseness is **who owns the talk**, not **whether there is a spine**.

### What you cannot copy in 3.5 hours at £400

| iVentiv condition | Four Corners condition | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Two days | 14:00–17:30 | No time for an empty morning while the agenda forms |
| Free, invitation is the product | Invoice same week, training/conference code | Buyer must know the problem before they ask finance |
| 40 Global Heads / VPs, ~100k-employee average firm | 30–50 Heads in 250–5,000 | Different problems, different stories that transfer |
| Pre-survey of a closed cohort | Cold-to-warm letter list | You cannot Pulse 400 people who have not booked |
| Peer facilitators from the same altitude | Two £500 contributors plus you | You *are* the facilitator. Speakers are provocations |

Going fully loose here would mean: skip the talks, open with “what’s on your desk,” and facilitate. That can be a brilliant **Corner**. It is a weak **first paid gathering**, because the letter has nothing to point at and a first-time room of 30 strangers will default to polite generalities.

### What to steal

1. **A three-line booking question:** “What’s the one L&D problem you would most like the room to work on?” You will not rebuild the afternoon around 40 answers. You will use it in the harvest and to brief the speakers. That is a Mini-Pulse. **Do this from date one.**
2. **Peer-owned tables.** Already in the run-of-show.
3. **Corners as the loose half.** Already the IP. Do not also make the talks loose. One half structured, one half emergent. That is the product.
4. **Short expert input, then conversation.** iVentiv says they avoid sit-and-listen. Your 20-minute talks are already that, *if* they frame a problem the tables can argue with.

**Verdict:** looser than a conference, tighter than iVentiv. Named problem on the ticket. Emergent Corners after. Not two keynotes hoping they will rhyme.

## 3. Recommended architecture for the first paid date

Keep the 14:00–17:30 machine. Change only the **brief to speakers**.

**Ticket sentence (one line):**  
*An afternoon for in-house Heads of L&D on why leadership programmes still don’t change managers — and what to do instead, with people in the same job.*

**Talk 1 (20 min):** the programme problem. Why another cohort, another 70:20:10 slide, another “manager as coach” module, still doesn’t show up in Monday morning 1-to-1s.

**Talk 2 (20 min):** the system around the manager. Time, incentives, skills, AI, what the organisation actually rewards. Must be in *argument* with Talk 1, not a new subject.

**Tables:** what would you stop funding this year. What would you protect. What would you need from the CEO that you have not asked for.

**Harvest:** Corners will likely be things like first-line managers, senior leaders, coaching culture, measurement. Let the room name them. Do not pre-print four topics.

**AI’s job on this date:** a lens (“what AI does to the manager’s job and to your content factory”), not Talk 2.

**Skills’ job on this date:** only as “you cannot become a skills-based organisation if managers still cannot develop people.”

This is still two talks. They are not loosely connected. They are one problem.

### The budget-code implication (assumption, test it)

- **Training code:** PTP / Hemsley for managers. Named skill. Easy PO.
- **Conference / professional development code:** LT, CIPD ACE, maybe Four Corners.
- **Consultancy / project code:** skills taxonomy, AI platform, leadership academy redesign.

Four Corners loses if it sounds like a **worse open course** (“we’ll teach you coaching”). It loses if it sounds like a **worse conference** (“two keynotes on hot topics”). It wins if it sounds like **the only half-day this year where people in your job will tell the truth about a live problem.**

Do not price or programme against “Conducting Challenging Conversations.” That is not your product. That is what your delegates *commission*.

## 6. Translate CLO topics down to a 250–5,000 Head of L&D

Same words. Different Monday.

| CLO / iVentiv meaning | Likely meaning in your room | Four Corners use |
|---|---|---|
| Reimagine the global leadership academy | The 12-person first-line programme still doesn’t change 1-to-1s | **Primary problem** |

| Skills-based organisation, marketplace, taxonomy | “Do we even know what skills we have?” Spreadsheet, not Workday | Secondary; avoid GSK-scale case studies |
| Learning culture with exec advocates | Managers still send people on courses instead of developing them | Same problem as leadership |
| Change management / org design around AI | The restructure already happened; L&D was told after | Possible Corner |
| People data / ROI | FD wants numbers; you have completions and happy sheets | Corner, not Talk 2 |
| Wellbeing | Already bought a provider; not their scarce afternoon | Do not headline |
| Employment Rights Act | People Director’s job; Head of L&D adjacent | Out of scope |

**Unknown, and worth the 15 calls:** which of these is *this year’s* live pain in 250–5,000 London employers. iVentiv data is the wrong sample. Until those calls exist, leadership-programmes-that-don’t-transfer is the safest spine because **every Head of L&D in that band has one**.

## 8. Kill-tests for the programme (not the P&L)

Any one is enough to change the date, not to add a third talk.

1. The two speakers cannot share a single question in one sentence.
2. Either talk needs more than 20 minutes “because the topic is big.”
3. The letter cannot name the afternoon without listing speaker names.
4. A booking from a 250–5,000 Head of L&D would be equally happy at Festival of Work for £0.
5. You rebuild 25 August (wellbeing + skills + famous names) and charge £400 for it.

## 9. What this means for the 6-week campaign

- Landing page: **problem + date + who is in the room + price.** Speakers in 15 words each, under the problem, not above it.
- Letter: one merge line, then the problem. Not “two loosely connected themes.”
- HubSpot email 2 can stay “the talks are 20 minutes; the tables are the point.” Add: both talks serve **one** question.
- Booking form: one optional field — “the problem you’d most like the room to work on.” That is the Mini-Pulse. Do not promise you will rebuild the agenda around it.
