# Four Corners product recommendations

Recorded 5 October 2026. The Four Corners fees below are working tests, not published products. Competitor fees are published facts from the databases. Sentences marked **Analysis** are design judgement. Where a price or an hour count was not on a page, it stays missing.

School prices are used only as product-shape evidence. Greenlight Web, Spectra, Gripped and Digital Litmus are model benchmarks. They are not evidence of what a training provider will pay.

## Free

### Contributor webinar

No direct competitor publishes a contributor webinar with a measured follow-on. Stickman’s free step is a two-minute health check, then a 20-minute call. AmbleGlow and McCracken use a free 30-minute call. Gripped uses a 30-minute session and then refuses a pilot.

**Analysis.** A webinar is a better first step than a free audit if the contributor is on trial and Four Corners needs demand evidence. The closest education mechanism is Stickman’s questionnaire, which exists to book the call. Recommended test: one webinar, a single next step, and a count of who books a review. Do not promise a second webinar in the public copy until the trial rule is decided internally. Risk: a webinar with no scored next step becomes a broadcast. Missing evidence: whether education buyers register for commercial webinars at all. Nobody in the set publishes registration numbers.

### Commercial Growth Review

McCracken’s review is from £1,500 and ends in a 90-day plan. His discovery call is free and is not the review. Skills Marketing’s audit is fixed-price and unpriced. Marketing 4 Success names a health check and does not price it.

**Analysis.** Call the free version a conversation with a boundary, and keep the paid diagnostic for the defined-service list. A free review that includes the 90-day plan gives away McCracken’s paid entry. Recommended test: 30 to 45 minutes, no written plan, one recommended paid product. Funnel position: after the webinar or the scorecard. Risk: scope creep into the £1,500-shaped product. Missing evidence: McCracken’s VAT and the hours inside his review.

### Commercial Growth Scorecard

Stickman’s health check is the only scored free tool in the education set. The buyer gets scores, hints and tips, not a long report. The next step is a call.

**Analysis.** Match that depth. A score and three prompts, then the review conversation. Do not add a written strategy. Recommended test: a short questionnaire with an automated score. Funnel position: before the review. Risk: if the score is vague, it will not justify the call. Missing evidence: Stickman does not show the scoring method.

### Four Corner Brief

No competitor publishes a named brief of this type. The nearest outputs are McCracken’s 90-day plan, which is paid, and Stickman’s plan, which is a three-month paid engagement with no fee shown.

**Analysis.** Keep the brief as a one-page readout from the review, not as a strategy. If it grows to a plan, it has become the diagnostic. Recommended test: one page, four observations, one suggested product. Funnel position: the artefact of the free review. Risk: buyers treat it as the whole engagement. Missing evidence: no competitor publishes a free brief, so there is no price analogue.

### Guides and checklists

ESS Assist sells the entry document at £49.99 + VAT rather than giving it away. Attenger sells a playbook at £697. Chameleon’s self-study course is £150 + VAT for about four hours.

**Analysis.** A free checklist is reasonable if it points at a workshop. A long guide is closer to ESS’s paid PDF or Attenger’s playbook, and should not be free if it replaces the workshop. Recommended test: one checklist, two pages, linked to one workshop. Funnel position: top. Risk: a library of free PDFs with no next product. Missing evidence: conversion from ESS’s guide is not published.

### Opportunity Insights

Blueberry, Projects and Beanstalk sell the conversations and do not give the data away. ESS sells tender alerts at £39.99 a month or £399 a year plus VAT, on a page not re-opened on 5 October 2026.

**Analysis.** A free sample of three opportunities can show the product. A feed is a subscription, and ESS already charges for one. Recommended test: a sample note on one employer segment, then a paid review or a partnership. Do not build a tracker unless the commercial model is the subscription. Risk: looking like a bid bulletin and being compared with £39.99. Missing evidence: whether training providers pay for opportunity data separately from sales time.

## Learning

### £295 half-day workshop

Attenger’s AI workshop is £350 per person for four hours, VAT status not published, verified 2 October 2026 and not re-opened on 5 October. ISA’s marketing day is £140 or £170, VAT status not published. AMCIS workshops were £185 or £285 on a lapsed date list. SDN’s six hours are £245 + VAT because the subject is funding numeracy for a group booking, not a per-person marketing day. Chameleon’s marketing masterclass is the closest school format and the price is withheld. The bid half-day is £250 on a blog, VAT status not published.

**Analysis.** £295 per person is inside the thin band of published half days. £295 per organisation would be a different product and is not evidenced. Recommended test: £295 per person, cap the group, state whether VAT is extra before the page goes live. Include an action list the delegate writes, because Attenger includes one and Chameleon includes one. Funnel position: first paid learning product. Risk: a per-organisation buyer compares it with a free webinar and does not come. Missing evidence: Attenger’s VAT, and whether ITPs pay per person. Attenger’s buyer is a school.

### £395 workshop plus implementation clinic

Nobody publishes this exact pair. SDN publishes the closest mechanism: £245 + VAT for the series, £350 + VAT if a one-hour call is added. Attenger’s four-week social courses are £995 or £1,295 per person for four live hours plus homework, which is a larger step.

**Analysis.** A £100 step for a clinic is modest next to SDN’s £105 + VAT step, and small next to Attenger’s jump from a half day to a four-week course. Recommended test: keep £395 as the same workshop plus one timed clinic, and publish the clinic length. If the clinic is an hour, say so. Funnel position: second learning rung. Risk: the clinic is so close in price that buyers skip it. Missing evidence: no education firm publishes this middle price.

### £595 workshop, clinic and follow-up review

Attenger’s self-paced ads course is £645, VAT status not published, with no live clinic. The SLT pack at £2,497 buys a 45-minute call and materials, which is a different object. Chameleon’s follow-up is often a term later and is not priced.

**Analysis.** £595 can hold a workshop, a clinic and a later review only if each piece has a time. Recommended test: publish the three contacts and the total live hours. Do not imply a written strategy at this price. McCracken’s strategy day starts at £1,250 and does include a written 90-day plan. Funnel position: top of the learning ladder, before a defined service. Risk: buyers expect the diagnostic. Missing evidence: no direct analogue at £595.

### Four-part implementation programme, and coaching between sessions

Stickman’s courses are six modules, workbooks and a weekly check-in, at £1,996 + VAT for two people on the sale page. Attenger’s social programmes are four weeks and four live hours, at £995 or £1,295 per person. HubGem’s masterclass is six calls plus implementation time at £1,175 + VAT. FE Business describes programmes, including one of four sessions, and does not price them.

**Analysis.** A four-part programme with coaching between sessions is closer to Stickman and Attenger than to a £295 workshop. Pricing it inside the workshop ladder will confuse it with a single afternoon. Recommended test: price it as its own product, above £595, and publish live hours and whether coaching is group or one to one. Funnel position: the bridge from learning to a sprint or a partnership. Risk: unlimited coaching. Stickman limits the contact to a weekly check-in of unpublished length. Missing evidence: Stickman’s check-in length, and FE Business’s fee.

## Defined services

### Commercial Growth Diagnostic

McCracken’s review is from £1,500 and produces a 90-day plan. Skills Marketing’s audit is fixed-price and unpriced, and produces a competitive position and a first-pass account list. A report title is not used.

**Analysis.** A diagnostic below £1,500 needs a smaller output than McCracken’s plan, or it will look underpriced against the only published education-adjacent review. Recommended test: a fixed fee, a named written output, and a stated hour cap. Do not use “from” unless the fee really moves. Funnel position: first defined service, after the workshop or instead of it for a senior buyer. Risk: giving the partnership away inside the diagnostic. Missing evidence: Skills Marketing’s audit fee and VAT.

### Competitor Marketing Review, Social Media Review, Website and Enquiry Review

Stickman’s three-month plan includes all three as ingredients, and the plan is not priced. Marketing 4 Success lists a website review and a mystery-shopper admissions assessment, unpriced. Attenger’s playbook teaches the buyer to do a monthly competitor look themselves.

**Analysis.** These three reviews are ingredients of Stickman’s plan. Sold separately, they need a short artefact each, or they collapse into the diagnostic. Recommended test: one review, one document, one fee, with the diagnostic as the bundle. Funnel position: side doors into the diagnostic or a sprint. Risk: three thin PDFs that do not justify a meeting. Missing evidence: no separate prices exist.

### Employer Proposition and Messaging, Employer Campaign Pack, Sales Process and Follow-Up Guide

Skills Marketing builds messaging inside the retainer and does not sell a pack. Projects, Beanstalk and Blueberry sell the calls, not the pack. Sticky Note sells campaign design and set-up from £100, with the media budget extra. That from-price is a production floor for schools.

**Analysis.** A campaign pack should say what the file contains and should not be priced like a £100 set-up unless the scope is truly that small. The sales-process guide is the piece FE Business trains and does not publish, and the piece Skills Marketing says is not yet a coaching product. Recommended test: fixed packs with a contents list. The campaign pack excludes media spend, in writing. Funnel position: outputs a partnership can later run, or a sprint can install. Risk: the pack is filed and nothing changes. Missing evidence: no ITP publishes a pack price.

### Pipeline and CRM Review, Employer Account Growth Review

HubGem’s priced CRM work is an onboarding from £10,560 + VAT, not a review. Digital Litmus, which is not education, starts HubSpot onboarding at £2,000 and RevOps at £1,500 a month. Those scopes are not shown to be equal. McCracken includes pipeline reporting inside the fractional role and does not sell a CRM review alone.

**Analysis.** A review can be a short diagnostic. An implementation cannot be priced like a review, because HubGem’s education build starts above £10,000 + VAT. Recommended test: the review produces a gap note and a recommended next step. Implementation is the sprint or HubGem’s market, not this line. Funnel position: defined service, with the sprint as the upsell. Risk: being asked to rebuild a portal inside a review fee. Missing evidence: nobody publishes a standalone CRM review price for providers.

### Practical Playbook

Attenger’s playbook is £697 one-time, VAT status not published, with a 90-day roadmap, templates and email support. It says the buyer does the implementation. The SLT pack at £2,497 adds 45 minutes.

**Analysis.** A playbook at a few hundred pounds is evidenced for schools, as a self-serve product. For training providers it is not evidenced. Recommended test: if the playbook is generic, price it as a product and say it is not done-for-you. If it is written for one provider, it is a defined service and should cost more than £697. Funnel position: either a digital product after a workshop, or a paid output of the diagnostic. Risk: copying Attenger’s school AI framing. The useful lesson is the boundary, not the words. Missing evidence: no ITP playbook price.

### Implementation Sprint

Gripped, not education, uses 30-day sprints inside a retainer and charges an onboarding fee of unpublished size. Digital Litmus uses 90-day sprints against points. No education firm publishes a sprint price. HubGem’s re-onboarding is eight weeks from £4,420 + VAT, which is a rebuild.

**Analysis.** A sprint should have a start, an end, a list of outputs and a rule that a portal build is out of scope. Recommended test: a fixed project of two to four weeks, priced above the diagnostic and below the first month of a partnership, with hours published. Funnel position: between defined services and Advisory. Risk: the sprint becomes unpaid onboarding for the retainer. Gripped charges onboarding separately and still asks for a term. Missing evidence: an education sprint price.

## Consultancy

### £179 an hour

ESS Assist’s product page is £120 for one hour and £220 for two, plus VAT, and the shop archive discounts a session to £99 + VAT. McCracken’s day floor is £500, which is not an hourly publication. No education marketing firm publishes £179 an hour.

**Analysis.** £179 is above ESS’s funding-advice hour and is not evidenced as a marketing hour. It can still be a working rate for small pieces if the page says what an hour buys. Recommended test: publish it as a working rate for defined extra work, not as the main product. Require a minimum of two hours so it does not undercut the diagnostic. Funnel position: overflow, not the front door. Risk: buyers assemble a cheap fractional director out of hours. Missing evidence: an ITP marketing hourly rate. McCracken sells days and months instead.

### £1,500 a day

McCracken’s interim floor is from £500 a day, VAT status not published, on a general page. Sticky Note’s on-site day is from £500, VAT status not published, for schools. Attenger does not publish a day rate. The £1,500 figure is not in the education sample.

**Analysis.** A £1,500 day needs a different output from McCracken’s £500 floor, and the page has to say what that output is. A facilitated strategy day with a written plan is the nearest shape, and McCracken prices that from £1,250 rather than as a naked day. Recommended test: sell a priced strategy day with a written plan, and keep £1,500 as the internal planning rate until a buyer-facing day has a named artefact. Risk: a buyer compares a silent day with a £500 floor. Missing evidence: any published education marketing day at or above £1,500.

## Growth Partnerships

### Advisory, £2,000 a month, about 24 hours

McCracken’s advisory floor is from £1,500 a month and does not include channel production. Greenlight Web, not education, sells £2,000 a month as up to 25 hours of mixed delivery. Skills Marketing’s cheapest department tier is from £3,500 a month and is already doing a channel. Spectra’s floor, not education, is from £3,000 a month for the full menu at a slow pace, on 12 months.

**Analysis.** £2,000 a month with about 24 hours is closer to Greenlight Web’s cap than to Skills Marketing’s Foundation, and closer to McCracken only if most of the 24 hours are leadership rather than production. The test split, about 12 hours of implementation, will look like a small managed service. Skills Marketing charges from £3,500 before it will run one channel, and it does not publish hours. Recommended test: describe Advisory as leadership, a monthly plan and a small amount of production, and publish the hour mix. Do not call it an outsourced department. That phrase belongs to the tiers above, and to Skills Marketing’s Growth and Full Department. Funnel position: first retainer, after a diagnostic or a sprint. Risk: 12 hours of implementation cannot sustain a campaign, and the client will compare the feeling of the work with Foundation at £3,500. Missing evidence: Skills Marketing’s hours, VAT and minimum term. Greenlight’s hours are not an education price.

### Supported, £4,000 a month, about 50 hours

Skills Marketing Foundation is from £3,500 and Growth is from £6,000. Greenlight Web’s middle tier is £3,500 for up to 45 hours, not education. Gripped’s channel execution starts from £7,500, not education. Spectra’s automation tier starts at £6,000, not education.

**Analysis.** £4,000 sits on top of the only published ITP floor and below that firm’s multi-channel tier. Fifty hours is more capacity than Greenlight Web publishes at £3,500, and far more than anyone has published inside an education fee. Recommended test: one primary commercial motion, weekly contact, and a written monthly output. Publish hours as a cap. Keep media spend outside. Funnel position: the main partnership. Risk: fifty hours at £4,000 is £80 an hour if the cap is used. That arithmetic is not a competitor’s rate. It is a delivery risk if the hours are senior. Missing evidence: no education firm publishes 50 hours inside a monthly fee.

### Managed, £8,000 a month, about 100 hours

Skills Marketing Full Department is from £8,500 a month, with board contact and an embedded fractional CMO. Hours are not published. Gripped’s full engine is from £15,000 a month, not education. Spectra’s named-lead tier starts from £12,000, not education. Greenlight Web does not publish a tier above £5,000 for 75 hours.

**Analysis.** £8,000 is the first Four Corners test that sits next to a published ITP department price. The hour assumption is the part no education firm has matched in public. One hundred hours is a delivery design, not a market fact. Recommended test: price Managed as the outsourced commercial function, name what is in scope, exclude media and software, and do not print 100 hours unless the team can show the mix of senior and production time. A from-price is honest if scope moves the fee, which is how Skills Marketing writes its ladder. Funnel position: account expansion after Supported, or the first sale where the provider has no marketing or sales capacity. Risk: promising a department and delivering a larger advisory month. Missing evidence: Skills Marketing’s hour mix, cancellation terms, and whether £8,500 is typical or only the floor.

## Cross-cutting rules

Publish VAT treatment before any test price goes on a page. The sample is split between “+ VAT”, silence, and one checkout that shows both.

Keep bid writing as an adjacent skill with its own fee. The evidenced bid prices are deposits and fixed applications, not months of marketing.

Do not describe a £2,000 month as a HubSpot implementation. The education build that is priced starts from £10,560 + VAT.

Separate the free score from the paid plan. The firms that publish a plan charge for it.

The contributor and partner model has two partial echoes. Stickman refers production to unnamed partners after the strategy fee. Attenger teaches a module for an association and uses that as trust language. Neither publishes a trial period, a two-webinar rule, or a promotion threshold. **Analysis.** Four Corners can keep the trial private and promote only people who have delivered. The public site should sell the client’s outcome, not the internal contributor ladder.
