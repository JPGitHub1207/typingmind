## How to read the prices

A figure is a fact only when an official page states it. `NOT PUBLISHED` means the page that was opened did not state it. A “from” price is not a fixed price. VAT wording is copied as published. Where a page is silent, the note is “VAT status NOT PUBLISHED”.

Paragraphs marked **Analysis** are judgement. They are not client results and they are not Four Corners prices.

## What they sell

Three commercial shapes appear in this batch. They should stay separate when Four Corners designs a ladder.

**1. Retained marketing department.** Skills Marketing sells a fixed-price Go-to-Market Audit, then monthly packages. Foundation is one lead-generation channel with fortnightly contact, aimed at smaller or earlier-stage providers. Growth adds multi-channel outbound, content, nurture and sales enablement, with weekly contact, and is described as an outsourced marketing department. Full Department adds brand, PR, events, campaigns, and an embedded fractional CMO with SLT and board involvement. A separate social ladder runs from a one-off Content Sprint from £1,800, through Channel Support from £2,200 a month and Channel Management from £3,800 a month, to Full Ownership from £6,000 a month. The account-based marketing page says coaching a provider’s business-development team is not currently core.

**2. Senior leadership without the channel team.** McCracken sells a part-time marketing director. The client’s employees and suppliers do the channel work. Entry products are a review and a strategy day, both ending in a written 90-day plan. Interim cover is a day rate. A free 30-minute discovery call precedes a fixed-price proposal. The site says there are no open-ended arrangements. The pound figures used here are the homepage and FAQ figures that were fully fetched. Other McCracken pages mention different floors and were not fully fetched. They are listed under unresolved evidence and are not treated as the price.

**3. Outsourced employer sales or telemarketing.** Projects offers meeting-booking for providers that already have a sales team, and fully outsourced apprenticeship sales through to commitment for smaller providers. Beanstalk sells fully managed campaigns: prospect data, callers, email and reporting, reached through a discovery call with an account director. Blueberry scopes employer-engagement calls to funding priorities and agrees success measures up front (qualified appointments, expressions of interest, placement or apprenticeship opportunities). Named channels are not separately priced.

**Training and CRM sit beside those three.** FE Business sells in-house sales programmes (solution selling, key account management, employer-engagement ambassadors) and wider employer-engagement programmes. One integrated programme is described as four sessions including an operational plan and KPIs. Outsourced and interim business development are named and not priced. MarCommEd sells tailored workshops, an 18-month marcomms apprenticeship mentoring offer with a 90-day plan and fortnightly mentor calls, and an MCE Engage community. Creative Collins sells a strategy workshop series, normally three to six sessions ending in a written strategy, then a marketing-management retainer bought as half-days or days. HubGem sells HubSpot onboarding, re-onboarding, an AI masterclass and an event.

## What is missing from their offers

**Analysis.** These are gaps relative to the Four Corners working list. They are not a claim that buyers have asked for the missing piece.

- No direct competitor in this batch publishes both a monthly fee and the expert hours inside it. Skills Marketing publishes the fee and withholds the hours. McCracken publishes days per month or days per week, and the two descriptions do not match.
- Skills Marketing’s own account-based marketing page says sales coaching for the provider’s business-development team is not currently core. FE Business trains that team and does not publish a price or a follow-on implementation clinic.
- Projects, Beanstalk and Blueberry sell the meeting or the sign-up. They do not publish a diagnostic, a messaging pack, a sales-process guide or a CRM review as a named product.
- McCracken does not sell the campaign. The homepage distinguishes a fractional CMO from an agency that executes channels.
- HubGem’s priced build starts above £10,000 + VAT. A £2,000 to £8,000 monthly partnership cannot be scoped as a HubSpot onboarding and still match this evidence.
- Nobody in this batch publishes a good/better/best learning ladder at £295, £395 and £595, or a priced implementation sprint sitting between a workshop and a retainer.
- Named education clients are thin. HubGem names TASIS England, The Alice Smith School, Access Creative Colleges and HIT Training Group. McCracken’s homepage testimonials name people and not their organisations. His training-provider page names prior employers and consultancy (Learning Curve Group, Apprentify Group, DPG, InterMinerva). One education case study is not treated as the whole customer base. Skills Marketing, Projects, Beanstalk and FE Business do not name clients on the pages used. Blueberry describes one specialist FE construction college and does not name it.

## Product ladders that are evidenced

**Skills Marketing.** Contact form, reply promised within one working day. Then a fixed-price Go-to-Market Audit whose amount is not published. Then Foundation, Growth or Full Department. Social work can be bought on its own ladder: Content Sprint, then Channel Support, Channel Management, Full Ownership. **Analysis.** The audit exists so the retainer is scoped. The social ladder lets a buyer start below the department price and still sit inside a monthly fee.

**McCracken.** Free 30-minute discovery call. Then a Strategy Day from £1,250 or a Review from £1,500, each with a written 90-day plan. Then a fixed-price fractional proposal, from £1,500 a month for advisory or £3,000 to £6,000 a month for the embedded description. Interim cover from £500 a day is a parallel door, not a step every buyer climbs. **Analysis.** The cheap diagnostic is still a four-figure project. It is not a free scorecard.

**Outsourced sales.** Beanstalk publishes a discovery call with an account director and then a proposal. Projects and Blueberry publish the service and a conversation. No free audit, no workshop and no public second step.

**HubGem, on the 2 October 2026 pages.** LIVE at £190 + VAT early bird, or a sales conversation. Then the AI masterclass at £1,175 + VAT, or onboarding from £10,560 + VAT. Re-onboarding from £4,420 + VAT is for teams that already have a portal. Monthly support is a later schedule, not a proven next step from the onboarding page.

## Operating shape

Skills Marketing’s Full Department is described as an embedded fractional CMO. Growth is described as the outsourced department. Meeting rhythm is published as fortnightly or weekly by tier. A named account-manager job title is not.

McCracken is one principal. He leads the relationship, writes the plan, and directs channel work done by others. Frances and Kevin is a separate CPD business he co-founded. It is not described as a delivery network for McCracken clients.

Beanstalk’s published relationship lead on the apprenticeship page is an account director at the discovery call. Blueberry agrees success measures before the calls start. A meeting calendar is not published.

Creative Collins prices the retainer as half-day or full-day capacity on a weekly, fortnightly or monthly rhythm. The rate is not published.

## Phase 2: schools, MATs and independent schools

These suppliers sell marketing, admissions or commercial income to schools. They are direct competitors under the brief. They are a different buyer from a training provider.

**Attenger** is the priced school ladder. On 5 October 2026 the homepage still offered the AI Playbook at £697 one-time, VAT status not published, and the playbook page priced the SLT Strategy Pack at £2,497, VAT status not published, with a 45-minute virtual implementation call and board-ready materials. The playbook page says the buyer does the implementation. Course prices were not re-opened on 5 October 2026: AI workshop £350 per person for 4 hours; admissions social programme £1,295 per person; commercial-team social programme £995 per person; Paid Ads for Schools £645 on Attenger’s own 2026 article. Consultancy, audits and done-for-you campaigns are named and not priced. Homepage result claims name Queen’s College Taunton, Clongowes Wood College, Repton School Enterprises, Edgbaston High School and Cheltenham Ladies’ College. A claim of 120+ organisations is not a client list.

**The Stickman Consultancy** still had its January-sale page live on 5 October 2026. Both the marketing course and the admissions course are £1,996 + VAT for up to two people, and £1,125 + VAT for each extra person. No crossed-out list price is shown, so this is a sale-page price. Each course is six modules, workbooks and a weekly live check-in whose length is not published. The free health check is a two-minute questionnaire with scores and tips, then a 20-minute call. The strategic marketing plan is three months and includes competitor, social, website and enquiry reviews. Its fee is not published. An optional second phase adds an action plan, monthly support and referrals to copy, photography and web partners.

**Chameleon** re-confirmed on 5 October 2026 that the Marketing Your School masterclass is contact-for-price. It includes templates, an action plan, a year of FundEd, and 15% off for ISBL members. Marketing Essentials remains the 2 October figure: £150 + VAT on the provider page, with a checkout that also shows £180 including VAT and calls £150 a launch offer against £225 + VAT. Winning Bids, not re-opened on 5 October, is £950 + VAT for a small school or the online version, and £1,250 + VAT for a regional group or MAT. The bid-writing half day is £250 on a provider blog, VAT status not published.

**Sticky Note Studio** published from-prices on 5 October 2026, VAT status not published: on-site creative session from £500 a day, admissions or recruitment or social campaign design and set-up from £100, website design or refresh from £1,000. The campaign budget is extra and set by the school. The Creative Partner retainer lists marketing, design, campaigns, advice and training, and does not publish a fee. The company number is still unresolved.

**Marketing 4 Success** lists health checks, mystery-shopper admissions reviews, campaigns, mentoring, a School Awards Strategy pack and a PLUS version, and ongoing support. No fee is on the homepage. A case-study page that appeared in search was not fully fetched, so a days-per-week figure from that snippet is not recorded.

**Tillison** sells SEO, paid search and paid social to independent schools and calls itself a growth partner. No fee. Testimonials name Portland Place School and a University of Portsmouth course launch. **AmbleGlow** sells school digital marketing, includes an Edumetric dashboard, and publishes a five-step onboarding path plus a free 30-minute discovery call. No fee. A company-number candidate was not confident enough to record.

**Analysis.** The school market already buys a free diagnostic, a few-hundred-pound workshop, a four-figure course or playbook, and an unpriced retainer or plan. Attenger’s £2,497 pack buys 45 minutes plus materials. Sticky Note’s from-£100 campaign line is a production floor, not a managed recruitment programme. None of these pages publishes expert hours inside a monthly school retainer.
