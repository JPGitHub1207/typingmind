# Four Corners event machine: is the format the company?

**Commercial decision memo**  
14 August 2026  
Object under investigation: a **repeatable premium professional-learning event**, not an L&D membership.

Evidence labels: **Verified** · **Calculated** · **Estimated / assumption** · **Unknown**

Prior Four Corners research is treated as **evidence**, not as a inherited verdict. The question has changed.

This is not a yes. It is also not the old no.

---

**WORKING CONCLUSION**

The educational architecture is a real product. It is closer to an **events company** than to a membership, a peer network, a training provider, or a multi-brand portfolio.

It can be a profitable practice **if, and only if**, a warm list fills a **paid** room.

It is **not** a clever undercut of a £500–£850 open training day. That comparison is the weakest part of the hypothesis. Those products do different jobs. Commodity one-day public courses in London already sell at **£490** (PTP). **Verified**, ptp.co.uk, August 2026 dates. Hemsley Fraser public programmes start at **£625 + VAT**. **Verified**, hemsleyfraser.com schedule 2025/26. CIPD Festival of Work 2026 is **free** in person. **Verified**. iVentiv is **free** for the senior L&D people the current site invites. **Verified**.

So: do not lead with “cheaper than a training day, better venue, better talks.” Lead with “a room of the right people, designed as learning, in a place you would actually want to spend an afternoon.” Price for contribution, not for a discount story.

**Do not launch Four Corners L&D + Coaches + Agency Owners + Founders as a portfolio in year 1.** One repeating paid date. Then clone the run-of-show, not the brand, and only into a vertical that does not already have the calendar.

**The test that matters:** 30 people pay **£400 + VAT** for a named weekday, eight weeks out, no complimentary inventory. The 25 August 2026 gathering cannot be that test. It is advertised as a **complimentary ticket**. **Verified**, fourcornersclub.co.uk.

## 1. What is being investigated (and what is not)

ChatGPT’s restatement is accepted as the brief:

> Can Four Corners develop a repeatable premium professional-learning event format that delivers considerably more educational, networking and experiential value than a conventional £500–£850 open training day, while charging perhaps £250–£500 per attendee and profitably repeating the format across carefully selected professional verticals?

That sentence contains **four claims**. They must be separated.

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A repeatable intimate format with talks → facilitated interrogation → lived experience can exist | **Yes.** You already describe it. Become’s 2026 coaching conference is a near-copy in another vertical. |
| It can be more valuable *as an experience* than a standard open course | **Sometimes, for a different job.** Not as a substitute for skill practice. |
| £250–£500 tickets can be profitable in a premium London room | **Yes at ~40 heads and network-rate speakers. No if you pay bureau keynotes. Fragile at 25 heads or £250.** See section 5. |
| The same blueprint can be deployed into several professional communities in year 1 | **No.** Production transfers. Audiences do not. Agency Hackers already runs the agency calendar. Become already runs the coach day. |

The unit of analysis is therefore **event-level contribution**, then **repeat purchase**, then **whether a second vertical is earned**.

It is not “will 10 Heads of L&D buy membership?”

## 2. USPs, values, and founder assets — taken seriously

These are inventoried in `data/usps_and_founder_assets.csv`. The ones that are actually scarce:

**Scarce (keep):** educational design after the talk; aggressive curation; hospitality as part of the learning; associate-trainer judgement about who can hold a room; willingness to stay small; independence (no stands, no data-selling) if you mean it.

**Not scarce:** “premium London venue.” Chiswell Street Dining Rooms is a good room. It is also a private-hire restaurant inside a hotel, with a published group menu from **£30 / £35** for two / three courses and a Samuel Room lunch **minimum spend of £4,000**. **Verified**, ETM menu PDF and Hire Space. Lots of people hire it.

**Not scarce:** “great speakers.” Charles Jennings and Petra Velzeboer are real draws. Jennings chairs iVentiv. Using him on a complimentary afternoon is goodwill, not a repeatable cost line.

**Founder capability that research must not dismiss:** you have run training organisations, managed associates for ~15 years, and believe you can get people in the room. Room-fill is the economic question. It is not dismissed. It is also **not proven at a price**. Complimentary RSVPs measure something else.

**Founder capability that is a risk if overextended:** “not wedded to one audience.” That is good optionality. It is a bad operating plan if it becomes four brands before one date repeats.

Values worth defending: conversation over speakers; trust over access; practitioner reality over polished success stories; no exhibition. Those conflict with unpaid contributors who are in the room to meet buyers. Say which you are.

## 3. Five economic models — genuinely separate

| Model | Who pays | Year-1 engine | Fit to this hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Events company** | Ticket (employer or individual) | Date-by-date contribution | **This is the company to test** |
| **Learning company** | L&D buying a skill outcome | Course utilisation, often CPD | Weak. You would be a worse Hemsley Fraser |
| **Membership company** | Annual belonging | Recurring fee whether they attend | Still a poor lead. Prior research stands as evidence |
| **Peer-network company** | The same 8–12 people | Corner retainer | Good *after* the event, not instead of it |

Prior conclusion that **membership is the wrong P&L** is kept as evidence. It does not kill an events company. iVentiv, Agency Hackers Live, School of Life, and Become are all events (sometimes with membership bolted on). They are not “communities that forgot to charge.”

iVentiv is the closest *format* analogue for senior L&D: max **40**, conversational, 18 years, 300+ events, **93%** satisfaction claimed. **Verified** as iVentiv’s public claims. Attendance is **free** because sponsors buy 1-2-1s and delegate lists. **Verified**, iventiv.com/sponsors and FAQ. That is a working events company with the opposite independence trade to yours. You cannot copy their fill method and keep “built for trust, not access.”

## 4. The open-course comparison, tested properly

James’s band (£500–£850 for an open training day) is a real market. It is not the only market, and it is not what Four Corners would replace.

| Product | Public price | What the buyer is hiring it to do |
|---|---|---|
| PTP London one-day public courses | **£490** | Skills practice in a City training room |
| Hemsley Fraser public | **from £625 + VAT**, plus Hub and a 30-min coaching call | Capability, toolkit, some follow-through |
| CIPD Festival of Work 2026 | **£0** in person | Volume content, exhibition, being seen |
| iVentiv Exchange | **£0** if you qualify | Peer room of 10,000+ employee VP/Directors |
| School of Life Festival of Ideas | **£210–£360** excl fees | Sunday ideas day, hospitality, brand |
| Four Corners 25 Aug | **£0** | Taste of the format |

A £350 Four Corners day can be *better hospitality and better conversation* than a PTP £490 skills course and still lose the booking, because the L&D admin is buying **“Conducting Challenging Conversations”** with a named outcome, not “an afternoon with interesting people.” Those budgets often sit in different pots (training vs conferences/networking). **Assumption**, but a common one; treat as unknown until you ask 15 buyers which code they would use.

Undercutting is therefore the wrong frame:

- You would be **above** PTP at £500 and **below** Hemsley Fraser at £350.
- You would be **above** every free L&D day that already exists for your current invite list.
- Premium venue + undercut ticket is how contribution dies (section 5).

If the day is genuinely educational, **price it with the training days you respect (£400–£500 + VAT)**, not with a discount narrative. Value is not a function of being cheaper.

## 5. Unit economics (the 40 × £350 example, and the grid)

All figures **ex-VAT**. Tickets modelled as corporate-ex-VAT. **Calculated** from the cost build in `data/cost_build.csv` and `data/event_unit_economics.csv`.

**Base package (the honest “amazing venue” full day):**

- Venue / hospitality: **£120 per person**, floor **£3,000** (Chiswell-class F&B, not a £79 DDR classroom)
- Two contributors at **network rate £750** each = **£1,500** (not bureau)
- Card/platform fees **5%**
- Other direct (AV extras, print, insurance slice) **£400**
- Founder labour **4 days × £1,000 = £4,000** opportunity cost, shown separately

ChatGPT’s sketch: 40 × £350 = **£14,000** revenue.

| | £ |
|---|---:|
| Revenue | 14,000 |
| Venue 40 × £120 | 4,800 |
| Speakers | 1,500 |
| Fees | 700 |
| Other | 400 |
| **Cash cost** | **7,400** |
| **Cash contribution** | **6,600** |
| After founder labour | **2,600** |

That arithmetic **works**. It is not a fantasy. Conditions:

1. **40 paid**, not 40 invited.
2. Speakers are **not** £3,000–£7,500 bureau keynotes. Two Band B specialists (**£3,000–£7,500** each, Speaker Agency 2026) drop contribution to **£2,100** cash and **negative** after founder time. **Calculated.**
3. CAC is near zero. At **£150** per attendee (cold ads), contribution collapses to **£600** cash. Warm list is mandatory.
4. You accept that £6,600 is **one date**, before tax, before a date that undersells, before a room hire that quotes above £120 pp.

**What the grid says (network-rate speakers, premium venue):**

| Heads \ Ticket | £200 | £300 | £350 | £400 | £500 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **25** cash contrib | −150 | 2,225 | 3,412 | 4,600 | 6,975 |
| **40** | 900 | 4,700 | 6,600 | 8,500 | 12,300 |
| **50** | — | — | 8,725 | — | — |
| **75** | — | — | 13,837 | — | — |

**Read it as an operator, not as optimism:**

- **£250 and 25 heads** is how you go broke politely. Do not.
- **£350 and 40** is the first number that looks like a business if fill is real.
- **£400–£500 and 40** is the default I would actually sell. It still undercuts Hemsley Fraser and does not pretend to be PTP.
- **75 heads** improves the spreadsheet and deletes the USP. iVentiv caps at 40 for a reason.
- Half-day at Chiswell min-spend **£4,000** plus network speakers still works at 40 × £350 (**£7,500** cash contribution). That matches the **current 14:30–17:00** shape better than a full open-course day. If you sell a half-day, do not compare it to a full training day at all.

**Year-1 practice maths (estimated):** 6 paid dates, 40 × £400, network speakers, warm fill, founder labour ignored as cash = 6 × £8,500 = **£51,000** contribution. After founder time (24 days) the firm paid you about **£1,100 a day**. That is a living if fill holds. It is not a scaled company. It is not four brands.

**How many vertical brands one central operation can support:** one founder, without a sales hire, **one vertical at 6–8 dates/year**, or **two verticals at quarterly** once the first list repeats. Shared: venue relationships, run-of-show, facilitation IP, email templates. Not shared: the list, the trusted names, the WhatsApp that actually fills. Informa/Ascential multi-brand economics require a sales team. You do not have one, and that is currently an advantage.

## 6. Room-fill — tested, not dismissed

You said you can get people in the room. That is the right variable.

What would count as evidence:

| Signal | What it proves | What it does not |
|---|---|---|
| 25 Aug complimentary fills | You can invite; the format is curious; Jennings/Petra/Boulting draw | Anyone will **pay** |
| LinkedIn DMs answered | Access | Budget |
| “I’d come to the next one” on the door | NPS | A card transaction |
| **30 paid tickets eight weeks out at £400 + VAT** | The business | Multi-vertical |

Paid professional events often convert **3–8%** of landing-page visitors and show **80–92%** of registrants. **Estimated** from industry write-ups, not your list. Invite-only warm lists convert much higher. Your model only works on the warm-list side. That fits your outreach skill. It does not fit a hope of “then we scale to coaches and founders with ads.”

Independence constraint: if fill depends on contributors bringing their own followers, those contributors are marketing. Price that honestly or refuse it.

| Vertical | Incumbent that already is the calendar | Ticket reality | Year-1 |

| **L&D / HR / OD** (your current site) | iVentiv free (big firms); CIPD FoW free; your own next date free | Employer can pay £400 if the room is mid-senior not iVentiv-eligible | **Prove paid fill here first** because the list and language exist |

| Trade bodies / contractor MDs | Their own conferences | Possible later as **white-label format**, not a consumer brand | Later |

The educational IP (contributor → idea → small-group interrogation → lived experience → named problem → continued conversation) **does** travel. The **audience** does not. Cloning the IP as a white-label for a trade association that already has a list is more rational than launching “Four Corners Coaches” as a public brand against Become.

## 8. What I am not being a yes-man about

1. **Caring about the user** is necessary and not a strategy. Become, iVentiv, School of Life and Agency Hackers also care, or at least perform care well enough to fill rooms.
2. **“Most events don’t focus on education enough”** is partly true and already being answered in-category (Become’s peer circles are the Corner by another name).
3. **Undercut + amazing venue + great speakers** cannot all be true if speakers are commercially priced. Pick two. The two that fit you are: **good room + educational design**, at a **serious ticket**, using **network contributors** you can actually direct.
4. The live site still sells **membership community + complimentary gathering**. That is the old object. A paid events company needs a different page and a different sentence.
5. Multi-brand “if scaled properly” is how operators go from one good afternoon to a thin company. Scale **dates in one list**, not logos.

## 9. What I am not inheriting from the old no

- I am not saying “nobody will pay to be in a room with peers.” They will. They already do, at School of Life (£210–£360 for a designed day) and at every Agency Hackers date.
- I am not saying the format is empty. The architecture is the IP.
- I am not saying you cannot fill a room. I am saying **complimentary fill is the wrong exam**.
- I am not saying Four Corners must stay a membership or must become Programme Decision Reviews. Those remain optional later products.

## 10. Recommended company, price, and kill-tests

**Company:** Four Corners as a **small events and learning-design practice**. One brand. One list until it repeats.

**Default offer:** weekday **half or full day**, 28–40 people, Chiswell-class room, two provoked conversations, facilitated interrogation, named takeaway, optional Corner for people who want to continue (priced separately, later).

**Default price:** **£400–£500 + VAT**, not £250. Employer-paid. No undercut story.

**Default contributor cost:** network / associate rate or expenses. If a name like Jennings is coming, treat it as a **launch gift**, not a cost model.

**Year-1 volume:** 4–6 dates. If 4 dates fail to average 30 paid, stop. If they average 35+ and NPS is high, add dates before adding verticals.

**Kill-tests (any one is enough to stop or reprice):**

1. Cannot get **30 paid** at £400 for the first charged date.
2. Need bureau speakers to fill.
3. Need sponsors, and you still want to claim independence.
4. The only way to “scale” is a 75-person room.
5. You feel the need to launch a second brand because the first list is thin. That is not scale. That is avoidance.

**15 conversations to have this month** (buyers, not speakers): “Would your organisation pay £400 + VAT for this specific afternoon, and which budget would it come from — training, conference, or your own card?” Count the budget code. That answers the open-course comparison better than this memo can.
