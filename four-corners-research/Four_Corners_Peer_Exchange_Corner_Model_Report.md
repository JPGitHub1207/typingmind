# Four Corners: Peer Exchange / Corner Model

**Independent commercial review**  
13 August 2026

This is a fresh assessment of a 10–12 person facilitated peer-group product (“a Corner”). It does not assume the previous Four Corners service list, price point or TAM framing is correct.

Evidence is labelled throughout:

- **Verified** — public primary source
- **Calculated** — arithmetic from verified figures
- **Estimated / assumption** — stated, not measured
- **Unknown** — needs primary research

Pricing, attendance and sponsorship figures are included only where a public source exists, or are explicitly marked as estimates.

**Live context.** As of this research, [fourcornersclub.co.uk](https://www.fourcornersclub.co.uk/) already describes an invitation-only community with quarterly gatherings and monthly Corners, a first listed event on 25 August 2026 at Chiswell Street Dining Rooms (EC1), complimentary tickets, and a pitch-free claim. Guest contributors named on the site include Charles Jennings (Chair of the iVentiv Advisory Board). This review treats that live prototype as the thing to test, not as proof the model works.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The Corner idea is commercially interesting and strategically dangerous in equal measure.

It is interesting because **intact, repeating groups of 10–12 peers** are a proven product in other professions (Vistage, Chief advisory groups, TAB). The job to be done is not “networking”. It is: *I am the only person in my organisation who does this job, I cannot talk honestly internally, and I need to steal working practice before I run the same programme again.*

It is dangerous because **that job is already partially served for free**, the **buyer is not a CEO**, and **sponsors pay for access to budget, not for intimacy**.

**What the evidence supports**

1. The UK people profession is large. CIPD reports **c.165,000** members globally and **144,577** in the UK (year to 30 June 2025). ONS APS analysis by CIPD found **217,300** HR managers and directors and **562,000** people in four HR-related occupations in 2019. That is not the addressable Corner market. Most of those people will never join a paid confidential group.
2. The commercially relevant set is **Heads / Directors** in organisations large enough to have a specialist function and small enough not to have a CLO office, Gartner seat or iVentiv invitation. That is a few thousand people, not hundreds of thousands.
3. Separate consumer brands for L&D, HR, Talent and OD would add cost without adding pricing power. **One community, tightly segmented Corners** is the only structure that matches how these people actually work (heavy overlap of challenges; thin specialist headcount).
4. **iVentiv-for-the-next-tier is likely to fail as a copy.** iVentiv’s sponsors buy 1:1 time with VP/Directors from **10,000+ employee** HQs. A Head of L&D in a 1,200-person organisation is a weaker economic object. Evanta (Gartner C-level Communities) already runs the scaled “free to CHRO, paid by sponsors, contacts shared” model. Four Corners would be a smaller, less defensible version.
5. **Willingness to pay £8,000–£15,000 is the wrong planning number** for this audience. CIPD practising membership is **£199–£261** a year. Vistage-level fees attach to CEOs. A realistic employer-paid test band for a Head of L&D Corner is **£750–£1,500 a year**, or **£50–£150 per session**, until interviews prove otherwise.
6. Sponsors will not pay material money for branding on 12 people with no names, no follow-up and no presence. The formats that do attract money (SHRM roundtable **$35,000** with badge scans and speaking rights) are exactly the formats that destroy the peer-trust product.

**Recommendation if investing personal capital**

Launch **one** London-accessible, **paid**, **L&D / Leadership / OD Head-level** Corner programme. Keep Corners **sponsor-free**. If cash is needed, sell **disclosed sponsorship of the quarterly gathering only**, with no attendee list export and no seat in the Corner. Do not launch four sub-brands. Do not make the first event free indefinitely. Do not put suppliers in the Circle.

**Year-1 target, not a fantasy plan:** 3 Corners, 30–36 members, £25,000–£55,000 revenue. That is a specialist practice, not a platform. It only becomes a business if 70%+ renew and a second city copies.

The model survives a kill-test on one condition: **continuity of the same 12 people, facilitated, confidential, local**. That is what Talent and Leadership Club (free, ~2,000 members, 35 countries, in-house only) and CIPD do not productise. If Four Corners cannot hold groups intact, it should stop.

## 2. Market Analysis

### 2.1 Two different markets must not be mixed

| Market | What it is | Why it misleads |
|---|---|---|
| People profession headcount | Hundreds of thousands of HR/L&D/admin/trainer roles | Most are not peer-group buyers |
| Specialist leader seats | Named Heads/Directors of L&D, Talent, OD, HR in organisations with a real function | Tiny, relationship-driven, diary-constrained |

Organisation counts (verified, Nomis UK Business Counts, March 2025):

| Size | UK enterprises (all legal statuses) |
|---|---:|
| 250–499 | 5,485 |
| 500–999 | 2,900 |
| 1,000+ | 3,030 |
| **250+** | **11,415** |

London + South East head offices: **4,250 (37%)**. England: **89%**.

DBT BPE 2025 private-only large businesses: **8,335**.

A Corner does not sell to “11,415 organisations”. It sells to **one named human** whose manager will approve time and, if paid, a purchase order.

### 2.2 What already occupies the diary

| Occupant | Price to the professional | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| CIPD membership | £199–£261 practising grades (2026/27) | Identity, qualification, branches |

| iVentiv | free if 10,000+ and VP/Director | Not the next tier |
| Evanta CHRO communities | free if C-suite and typically $500m+ | Sponsor-funded; T&Cs share contacts with sponsors |
| LinkedIn | £0 | Default informal peer advice |

**Verified implication:** the market is not empty. The empty slot, if it exists, is **a closed repeating group**, not “a community”.

### 2.3 Demand signals (what is evidenced vs hoped)

**Evidenced**

- CIPD Learning at Work 2023: 53% of L&D-function respondents reported increased workload. Capacity, not lack of events, is the constraint.
- CIPD 2020: in large organisations L&D owns the learning budget in **31%** of cases. Employer payment is not automatic.
- CIPD Good Work Index 2025 (via CIPD): more than a third of working adults sometimes/often/always lonely at work; “lonely at the top” is discussed for leaders. This is **general workforce loneliness**, not a measured L&D-peer-group demand.
- iVentiv’s 18-year existence and claimed 93% satisfaction show **some** senior L&D/Talent leaders value structured peer exchange — at a very different seniority and company size.
- Vistage, Chief and TAB’s longevity show **paid** small groups work when the member has P&L or a company-sponsored executive development budget.

**Not evidenced (unknown)**

- Whether a UK Head of L&D will pay £750–£1,500, or only attend if free.
- Whether their HRD will approve either.
- Whether they will protect six dates a year.
- Whether they prefer role-matched or challenge-matched groups.

Do not treat event RSVPs to a complimentary EC1 afternoon as willingness to pay.

## 3. Audience Analysis

SOC codes do not equal Four Corners audiences. “Vocational trainers” includes warehouse assessors. “HR managers and directors” includes HR Business Partners in 80-person firms. Figures below separate **occupation stock** from **Corner-eligible leaders**.

### 3.1 Occupation stock (dated, verified)

CIPD analysis of ONS Annual Population Survey (SOC 2010), UK:

| Occupation | 2011 | 2019 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1135 Human resource managers and directors | 131,100 | **217,300** |
| Four HR-related SOCs combined (managers, officers, trainers, HR admin) | 438,000 | **562,000** |

Trainers “hardly increased at all” over that period (CIPD). Job-title inflation in “HR manager” is explicitly flagged.

**Gap:** this project did not obtain 2024/25 4-digit APS extracts (Nomis NM_218_1 query failed on encoding). Treat 2019 as the last clean published bundle. Directionally the profession grew; APS quality has since been criticised. **Unknown: current 4-digit counts.**

ASHE 2025 (SOC 2020 1136 Human resource managers and directors), as compiled from ONS: UK median **£54,474**; London median **£66,725**. A £1,200 membership is ~2% of median UK pay, ~1.8% of London — plausible as an employer training expense, painful as a personal expense.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L&D leaders** | 4,000–8,000 Heads/Directors; many more managers | 500+ for a true Head | **Head / Director** | Easy on LinkedIn by title | T&L Club, LT, CIPD L&D, Fosway (tech), iVentiv (giants) | Medium if budget line exists; low if personal | Local F2F wins for trust; online for geography |
| **HR / People leaders** | 8,000–11,000 HRD/People Director equivalents in 250+; far more HR Managers | 250+ | **HRD / People Director**; HR Managers are too numerous and heterogeneous | Easy | CIPD, HRD media roundtables, Evanta (C-suite), Festival of Work | Medium–high at HRD; low at manager | F2F if exclusive; otherwise crowded |
| **Talent leaders** | 2,500–5,000 named Heads of Talent | Often 1,000+; combined with L&D below that | Head of Talent / Succession | Medium (title scatter: Talent, S&L, Capability) | T&L Club, TAP, assessment-vendor clubs | Medium | Same as L&D |
| **OD leaders** | 1,500–4,000 in-house OD leads | 1,000+ or change programmes | OD Manager to Director | Harder (title scatter; many consultants) | CIPD OD&D network (new national), Roffey Park alumni, OD Network ($200–$300, mostly US) | Medium; values-led | F2F strongly preferred; consultants must be filtered |
| **People partners / HRMs** | tens of thousands | any | Manager | Easy | CIPD branches | Low | Not a Corner product |

**Commercially attractive level: Head / Director, not Manager, not C-suite.**

- **Manager:** large numbers, weak budget, CIPD already serves, high churn.
- **C-suite CHRO:** Evanta, Gartner, World50. Four Corners cannot win on prestige.
- **Head / Director:** enough authority to bring real cases; enough isolation; not already farmed by Evanta/iVentiv.

### 3.3 Function-by-function commercial verdict

**L&D — strongest launch audience.** Isolated specialists, recurring practice problems (manager capability, transfer, evaluation, AI in learning), identifiable titles, weaker C-suite community coverage than HR. Diary already includes LT once a year — a Corner is a different job.

**OD — strongest unmet-need emotionally, weakest commercially.** Thin population, title chaos, consultant contamination, CIPD now organising them nationally. Good as a Corner *theme* and as mixed groups, poor as a standalone P&L in year 1.

**Talent — real but small.** Succession and HiPo are excellent Corner topics. Many “Heads of Talent” are the same humans as Heads of L&D. Separate brand = split of an already small room.

**HR / People — largest, most competed.** HRD roundtables are a commodity. Only worth a Corner if gated to non-competing sectors and a tight challenge (e.g. org design in 1,000–5,000 employee firms). Not the first brand.

**People Partners — do not launch.** Volume vanity.

### 3.4 Geography

Launch geography is already chosen in the live site: **Moorgate / EC1**. That matches HQ concentration. It also confines the paid product to people who can reach the City on a weekday afternoon. Online Corners expand reach and weaken the only differentiator versus T&L Club.

**Assumption:** a London F2F core with optional online catch-up is more attractive to the EC1-invited set than a Zoom-first national product. **Unknown** for Manchester, Birmingham, Edinburgh. Do not open a second city until one London Corner renews.

### 4.1 What actually makes a small group a business

| Engine | Examples | Why it works | Failure mode if copied blindly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Member pays a lot because they are the P&L owner | Vistage, YPO, TAB | Fee is trivial vs a CEO decision | Head of L&D is not a CEO |

### 4.2 Selected analogues (facts only where sourced)

**Talent and Leadership Club** — Founded 2010. Nearly 2,000 members, 35 countries. In-house only, no consultants. No cost. Partnerships: thought partners at events, leading smaller gatherings, commissioning research. **This is the product Four Corners most resembles if Corners are open, mixed and free.**

**iVentiv** — See section 5.

**Evanta / Gartner C-level Communities** — Complimentary to qualifying C-suite (Fortune/Yahoo 2024: typically $500m+ revenue; exceptions possible). 13 CHRO communities, 2,500+ CHROs claimed. Summits, Inner Circle dinners, virtual Town Halls. Application T&Cs include sharing contact details with **peers and sponsors**. 400+ sponsorable events a year claimed. Sponsor value stated as C-level access, demand creation, deal acceleration. **Price to sponsors: not published.**

**World50 / HR50** — Invitation; ~$1bn revenue filter. Secondary sources report ~$50,000/year and ~5,000 members across C-suite communities. **Not a published price list.**

**Chief** — 2024 Bizjournals: packages from **$5,900** and coaching from **$7,500**; often employer-sponsored; advisory groups of 8–12. 2026 secondary commentary still clusters around those numbers. Treat as **indicative**.

**Vistage** — US CEO dues ~$16,500 plus ~$2,500 initiation (independent reviews, 2026). **UK CEO list price not verified.** UK Emerging Leaders product sheet: **£3,600/year**. Chair model: independent contractors; £3,000 one-off to start a Chair practice (Vistage UK member FAQ).

**Fosway Corporate Membership** — Professional / Premium / free Associate. Roundtables, 9-Grid detail, analyst time. Prices unpublished.

**CIPD** — 144,577 UK members. Practising fees **£199–£261**. National OD&D network open to members. Branches are the local peer layer.

**SHRM 2026** — Roundtable Session Sponsor **$35,000**: logo, up to three minutes’ opening remarks, **sponsor facilitates**, **badge scans shared**. This is the honest sponsor product. It is the opposite of a pitch-free Corner.

**FETC 2025** — Leadership Roundtables **$15,950**.

**HRO Today Forum 2026** — Bronze from **$10,000** (standard); start-up bronze from $5,000.

**Gartner CHRO (G-Cloud)** — **£24,100** per licence per year for one published public-sector SKU.

### 4.3 Innovative vs merely famous

Genuinely distinctive mechanics: Evanta’s local C-suite cadence + sponsor T&Cs; iVentiv’s gated 40-person exchange + mandatory-feeling 1:1s; Vistage’s paid Chair + monthly full day; T&L Club’s hard in-house filter at zero price.

Not innovative: another “senior HR roundtable with a guest speaker in a London dining room”. That market is full.

## 5. iVentiv Deep Dive

### 5.1 Verified mechanics (iventiv.com)

| Item | Public fact |
|---|---|
| Founded | 2008 (Russell Butler); site also says 18 years / 300+ events |
| Member price | **Free** for approved VP/Director (or equivalent) from companies with **10,000+ employees**, HQ-based, global/regional functional remit |
| Travel | Not covered; venue rate negotiated |
| Cap | **Maximum 40** executive-level participants |
| Why free | “Thanks to the support of our trusted event partners” |
| 1:1s | Maximum **two or three** 1:1 sessions per day; delegates indicate preferred partners |
| Sponsor role | Sponsor delegates “participate in the whole event **as an equal**”; 1:1s with decision-makers; Plenary packages include a **60-minute plenary with all delegates** (sponsorship-availability page) |
| Packaging | Plenary and Business Development packages; “all include guaranteed 1:1s”; book **8+ in-person packages** for annual sponsor status |
| Sell-through | “Regularly fully booked 12 months in advance” |
| Satisfaction | All-time **93%** (about page); FAQ says in excess of **92%** |
| Topics | Pre-event questions shape the agenda; Advisory Board including Charles Jennings |
| Substitution | Colleague of same seniority may attend |
| Acquisition | Word of mouth and direct contact; “why have I not heard of iVentiv” is an FAQ |

**Unpublished (do not invent):** sponsor fee per event; number of sponsors per event; revenue; conversion from 1:1 to contract; true mix of CLOs vs more junior “Directors”.

### 5.2 Why executives attend for free

They get a rare room (same level, huge companies), interactive format rather than a conference, and the price is zero plus their time and travel. The 1:1s are the tax. iVentiv keeps perceived quality by: hard size gate; approval; small n; agenda from participants; framing sponsors as “partners” and “equals” not booth staff. GP Strategies (sponsor since 2009) and Coursera quotes on the sponsor page celebrate being *inside* the conversation.

### 5.3 Why suppliers finance it

Public sponsor quotes emphasise **access**, not branding: “break down the supplier/client barrier”; “included in the sessions as a participant”. Guaranteed 1:1s with people who can buy enterprise learning/talent products is a pipeline tool. A single CLO deal at a 10,000+ employer can justify a high five-figure event cost. **That arithmetic is why the gate is 10,000 employees.**

### 5.4 Could “iVentiv for the next tier” work?

**Target hypothesised:** 500–10,000 employees; Head/Manager/Director; smaller groups; more frequent; local; shorter; more continuity.

**Structural reasons it could work**

- That population is excluded from iVentiv and usually from Evanta.
- They are more numerous.
- Local, shorter, repeating meetings fit their travel budget.
- Leadership boutiques and mid-market HR tech might value a cheaper, more frequent touch.

**Structural reasons it is likely to fail (stronger)**

1. **Sponsor ACV collapse.** A Degreed/Coursera/GP Strategies enterprise deal at a global 40,000-person company is a different ROI object from a £40k leadership programme at a 900-person manufacturer. Sponsors who fund iVentiv will not automatically fund the next tier at a proportional price. Many will simply decline.
2. **The next-tier buyer is not the economic buyer.** CIPD: L&D owns budget in 31% of large organisations. A 1:1 with a Head of L&D in a 700-person firm often cannot convert.
3. **Frequency fights the iVentiv magic.** iVentiv is a special occasion. Monthly local meetings become “another Teams”. Continuity is good for members and bad for sponsors (they have already met everyone).
4. **Evanta already owns ‘free + local + HR’ at the top.** Going down-market without a new economic engine is just a weaker Evanta.
5. **Quality gate is the product.** Relaxing 10,000+ and VP-level is how the room fills with providers’ prospects who cannot buy. Sponsors notice. Members notice.
6. **18 years and word-of-mouth.** iVentiv’s switching costs are social. A new local copy has neither density nor myth.

**Verdict:** do not build iVentiv-lite. Steal **gating, participant-set agenda, small n**. Do not steal **free + sponsor-in-the-room + 1:1s** unless you can name sponsors whose ACV matches the next tier *and* members who accept the tax. That is a validation question, not a strategy.

## 6. Model A — Paid Corners

### 6.1 Price bands to take seriously

| Band | Analogues | Fit for Head of L&D |
|---|---|---|
| £50–£150 per meeting | workshop / CIPD branch event | Possible personal spend; weak commitment |
| £500–£2,000 per year | CIPD x3–10; Vistage Emerging Leaders is £3,600 | **Primary test band** |
| £3,600–£8,000 | Vistage Emerging Leaders; Chief core (USD) | Only if employer has an exec-dev budget and the group feels elite |
| £8,000–£15,000 | Gartner-ish / Vistage CEO-adjacent | **Do not assume.** Wrong reference class for this buyer |

**Assumption, not fact:** employer-paid **£1,200/year** (six sessions) is the base case to test. It is ~£200 per session, in the same order as a decent open programme day, far below Vistage CEO, far above CIPD.

### 6.2 Unit economics (illustrative)

Assumptions marked. Venue £90pp F2F (estimate). Two F2F + four 90-minute online meetings per year. Founder facilitates. Tools £3k/year. No salary except a founder draw.

**One Corner, 12 members, £1,200:** revenue £14,400. Cash costs: 2 × 12 × £90 venue £2,160; Zoom/CRM share £400; incidentals £400. **Cash contribution ~£11,400 before founder time.** If founder time is 12 days/year fully loaded at £500/day, economic profit ~£5,400 per Corner.

That looks attractive and is a trap. It ignores **acquisition**. If 15 qualified conversations yield one member (unknown; 8% used in the sheet as an optimistic placeholder), 12 members need ~150 conversations. That is months of founder BD before the group exists. No-shows and replacements destroy intimacy.

**Groups required for a living:** at £1,200 and founder-delivered, **8–12 Corners (100–140 members)** to support a modest full-time income after tax, insurance, and churn. See `data/financial_scenarios.csv`.

**Renewal:** below ~70% annual renewal the BD treadmill never stops. Unknown.

**Gross margin on cash costs:** can be 60–80% at small scale. **That is not the same as a viable company.**

## 7. Model B — Free Sponsored Corners

### 7.1 Formats tested

| Format | Member perception | Sponsor attractiveness | Trust | Likely fee (est.) | Scalable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sponsor outside the room; branding + anonymised insight | Best | Weak unless insight is specific | High if disclosed | Low hundreds to ~£1.5k | Poor. Sponsor asks “why not just fund a webinar?” |
| Sponsor inside as “equal”; no pitch | Members assume a pitch anyway | High (this is iVentiv/Evanta) | Low–medium | Only works if ACV is large | This *is* iVentiv’s model |
| Expert sponsor, leaves before confidential segment | Acceptable if timed honestly | Medium | Medium | Maybe £2–4k per Corner | Operationally fiddly |
| Sponsor + client case, no selling | Looks like a case study webinar | Medium | Medium–low | Similar to content sponsorship | Blurs into vendor marketing |

**Sponsor ROI they actually need (not branding):** meetings with people who can buy in the next 12 months; permission to follow up; signal that Four Corners will not block a commercial relationship. Badge scans and 1:1s are the products in the public price lists (SHRM $35k, FETC $16k).

**If Four Corners refuses names, follow-up and presence, rational sponsorship for 12 people is close to zero.** Do not build a P&L on hope.

**Regulatory / privacy:** UK GDPR. Sharing attendee data with a sponsor without consent is unlawful. Evanta solves this by putting it in the T&Cs up front. That is a trust choice, not a paperwork detail.

**Member trust:** one supplier in 12 can be metabolised if the facilitator is fierce and the supplier is a practitioner. It will not survive a second supplier, a slide, or a follow-up email the member did not expect.

## 8. Hybrid Models

Plausible versions (economics in the CSV):

1. **£750/year membership + sponsor-funded quarterly gathering** (recommended hybrid shape). Corners remain closed. Gathering is the sponsor inventory.
2. **£25–£100 per F2F attendance + free online** — trains people to skip F2F or skip payment. Weak.
3. **Organisational membership (£2,000–£4,000) for two seats** — only if HRD is the buyer. Unknown demand.
4. **Free online Corners + paid F2F** — high risk of destroying paid conversion. Must A/B test, not assume.
5. **Paid Corners + free sponsored annual event** — good acquisition; event must not leak Corner confidentiality.

Hybrid does **not** mean “members pay a bit and sponsors sit in the Corner”. That combines the trust hit of B with the conversion friction of A.

## 9. Function-specific Community Analysis

| Design | Relevance | Complexity | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Four consumer brands (FC L&D / HR / Talent / OD) | Higher on paper | Website, CRM, sponsors, facilitators ×4 | **Kill for launch.** Premature |
| One brand, Corners segmented by **role** | High if titles are clean | Matching load | Good |
| One brand, Corners segmented by **challenge** | Highest session quality | Members float between Corners; continuity dies | Use for quarterly gathering themes, not as the core group |
| Broad community, tight Corners | Best of both | Needs a real matching function | **Recommended** |

Overlap is not a branding problem. A Head of Talent in a 1,500-person firm *is* often the Head of L&D. Forcing them to pick a sub-brand loses them.

**Launch first: L&D / Leadership capability Heads.** Then mix OD and Talent into challenge-based gatherings. HR/People Directors as a second Corner type only after one L&D Corner renews.

**Consultants:** T&L Club’s in-house-only rule is the correct default. OD is full of independents who will dominate airtime. **In-house only** unless running a paid consultant Corner later (different product, different ethics).

## 10. Small Corner vs Large Event

| | Corner-first (12, repeating) | Event-first (40–100) | Mixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trust / confidentiality | High | Low–medium | High if walls hold |
| Sponsor proposition | Weak unless rules are Evanta-like | Stronger | Gathering is the inventory |
| Acquisition | Slow, personal | Faster | Event feeds Corners |
| Ops | Matching, replacements, 12 diaries | Venue, run-of-show | Both |
| Competition | Fewer true copies | Saturated London HR breakfasts | — |
| Retention | Can be high if group gels | Event-hopping | Corners hold; events recruit |

**Mixed is stronger than either alone**, with a hard wall: **Corners = members only; events = acquisition + optional sponsor.** The live website already sketches this (quarterly gatherings + monthly Corners). The commercial error would be making the gathering the product and the Corner an unkept promise.

Attendance volatility: a 12-person group with three no-shows is a failed meeting. Paid + calendar holds + waitlist are not optional.

## 11. Sponsor Economics

### 11.1 What public prices actually are

| Product | Public price | What the sponsor gets |
|---|---|---|
| SHRM26 roundtable | **$35,000** | Remarks, facilitation rights, **badge scans** |
| FETC leadership roundtables | **$15,950** | Conference roundtable inventory |
| HRO Today bronze | **from $10,000** | Forum branding packages |
| iVentiv / Evanta / Fosway vendor | **not published** | Access; iVentiv: 1:1s + equal participation |

UK Learning Technologies exhibitor list prices were not captured as a public rate card in this pass (**unknown**).

### 11.2 Rational spend vs Corner inventory (estimated)

A leadership provider’s customer lifetime value on a mid-market account might be **£20k–£150k** over two years (**assumption**, not a survey). Rational customer-acquisition spend at 10–20% of first-year revenue is **£2k–£15k** per *won* deal. Paying £2,000 to be in a room of 12 *unqualified* Heads with no follow-up is irrational. Paying £2,000 for **two warm, consented conversations** with Heads who have a live RFP can be rational.

| Inventory | What a rational supplier might pay | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| 12 L&D Heads, no names, no presence | ~£0–£1,000 | Insight report must be actually useful |
| 12 L&D Heads, one in-room silent observer | **Unknown**; test £2,000–£5,000 | Members pre-consent |
| 12 L&D Heads + consented 1:1s | Could approach iVentiv logic at a discount | This *is* selling access |
| 40-person gathering, pitch-free, no list | **£3,000–£8,000** estimate | Guest slot or branded drinks |
| 40-person gathering with 1:1s and list | **£8,000–£20,000** estimate | Members will notice |
| 100-person | Approaches ordinary conference sponsorship; compete with LT | Probably not Four Corners’ game |

**Category exclusivity** is how you raise price and how you capture the market with one LMS. Avoid in year 1.

**HR tech vs leadership boutiques:** tech vendors think in pipeline and MQLs (Evanta/G2 logic). Boutiques think in relationships. Boutiques are the more natural Corner sponsors *if any* — and they are the ones members most fear.

**Do not assume suppliers will pay for branding.** The public rate cards buy **data and talk time**.

## 12. Trust / Independence Framework

If suppliers are anywhere near members, copy the strictest rules that still allow a gathering sponsor:

1. Corners: **zero suppliers**. No exceptions in year 1.
2. Gatherings: **maximum one sponsor**, members ≥10:1, **no slides, no demo, no pitch**.
3. Sponsor does **not** choose attendees or agenda.
4. No attendee list, no email export, no CRM sync. Opt-in 1:1s only, after the event, via Four Corners.
5. Disclosure on the invite: who paid for the canapés.
6. Facilitator can remove the sponsor from the room in the moment.
7. No exclusivity over “what good looks like”.
8. Charles Jennings / iVentiv-adjacent guests are **content**, not a channel for iVentiv sponsors — make that explicit, because the live site already creates the association.
9. In-house practitioners only.
10. Publish a one-page charter before the first paid invoice.

Communities that hold the tension: iVentiv (by making the tax visible and the gate high); T&L Club (partners at the edge, members free and in-house); Vistage (speakers, not vendors, as the default). Communities that resolve it by surrendering: Evanta (contacts shared with sponsors in T&Cs). **Choose a side.**

**The line:** money may buy a gathering, never a Corner, never a conclusion, never a name.

## 18. 30-Day Validation Plan

Run **before** another complimentary gathering becomes the brand.

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1–10 | 25 interviews: 8 L&D Heads, 5 Talent, 5 OD, 5 HRDs, 2 HR Managers. No pitch. Record last peer group used, last fee paid, six-date willingness, F2F vs Zoom, in-house-only preference |
| 1–10 | 10 sponsor interviews: 4 leadership providers, 3 HR tech, 2 coaches, 1 business school. Show four packages (outside / inside / expert-leaves / gathering-only). Ask **willingness to pay a number** |
| 11–18 | Two written offers to the same 40 qualified names, randomised: (A) £1,200/year Corner, sponsor-free; (B) free Corner, one disclosed in-room sponsor. Measure replies, not compliments |
| 19–26 | Pilot **one 90-minute online Corner** with 8–10 of the highest-intent names. Charge £0 but collect card for a £99 “hold” refundable on attendance (tests seriousness) |
| 27–30 | One F2F vs online preference vote after the pilot. Kill or proceed |

**Pass/fail (logic, not theatre)**

The denominators are small because the market is relationship-based. Thresholds are set so that *failure is cheaper than a year of founder subsidy*.

1. **Member demand:** of 25 qualified interviews, **fewer than 8** say they would attend six sessions in 12 months → the diary problem is fatal; stop. (8/25 = 32%. Below that, even a heroic closer cannot fill 3 Corners.)
2. **Willingness to pay:** of those 8+, **fewer than 4** accept employer-paid **≥ £750/year** or personal **≥ £400/year** → paid Model A fails; only proceed if sponsors pass test 4.
3. **Offer test:** of 20 people receiving offer A, **fewer than 3** explicit yes (PO or calendar + invoice permission) → do not price as a membership business yet.
4. **Sponsor test:** of 10 supplier conversations, **fewer than 2** will name **≥ £4,000** for a 40-person gathering **without** a list, **or ≥ £2,000** for in-Corner presence **with** member consent → sponsor-funded model fails as a primary engine.
5. **Pilot:** **fewer than 7 of 10** hold the date and stay 70+ minutes → facilitation/format fails.
6. **Free vs paid:** if offer B (free+sponsor) converts at **>3×** offer A and sponsors fail test 4, you have a hobby, not a firm.

## Questions for James

Ranked by how much a true answer would change the recommendation. None of these were knowable from public sources.

1. **Who is paying for the 25 August 2026 EC1 gathering — you, a sponsor, or a guest contributor’s firm?** If a sponsor already exists, the independence charter is not theoretical; it is a current conflict.
2. **Will you refuse revenue that requires suppliers in the Corner, even if that means the business stays below £50k in year 1?** A yes locks Model A/E. A no means we should design Evanta-lite honestly, not pitch-free theatre.
3. **Are consultants and providers excluded from membership in practice, or only from “pitching”?** T&L Club’s hard in-house rule is a strategy choice. Admitting independents changes OD Corners completely.
4. **Is your personal objective a specialist living (~£80–150k revenue by year 3), a saleable media/sponsor platform, or a multi-city brand?** The operating model at 10 Corners versus 100 is a different company.
5. **Can you name 40 specific Heads of L&D within reach of EC1 who already take your calls?** If the list is shorter than 40, acquisition cost dominates every spreadsheet.
6. **What did invited people actually do with previous Four Corners / network invitations — RSVP rates, attendance, and whether anyone offered to pay?** Real conversion beats analogue pricing.
7. **Will you invoice the employer, the individual, or keep it complimentary until “the community feels ready”?** The last option is how free becomes the price.
8. **Are you willing to run L&D-only for 12 months and tell HR/Talent/OD they are welcome as individuals, not as brands?** If not, we should price the complexity of four communities now.
9. **What relationship, if any, exists with iVentiv beyond Charles Jennings appearing as a guest?** Shared DNA helps content and hurts “we are not a supplier room”.
10. **If 4 of 25 qualified Heads will pay £750 and 0 of 10 sponsors will pay £4,000 without a list, do you stop — or self-fund a hobby?** The validation plan only works if you will obey it.
