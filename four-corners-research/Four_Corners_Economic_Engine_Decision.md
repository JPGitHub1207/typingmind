# Four Corners: What is the single economic engine?

**Decision memo**  
13 August 2026  
Responds to a challenge that the Corner report still treated Four Corners as a membership business.

Evidence labels:

- **Verified** — public primary source
- **Calculated** — arithmetic from verified figures
- **Estimated / assumption** — stated
- **Unknown** — needs primary research

This memo does not replace the Corner report’s market sizing. It replaces the **recommendation of what company to build**.

---

## 1. The challenge, in one page

The Corner report asked: *would people pay to belong to a 10-person peer group?*

That was the wrong primary question.

The better questions are:

1. **Where is value created?**
2. **Who writes the cheque for that value?**
3. **What is the one cash engine in the next 18 months?**
4. **What must Four Corners refuse, so the engine is not contradicted?**

A subsequent critique (via ChatGPT) made three claims. Two are substantially right. One is right about value and wrong about the firm.

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Four Corners is being treated as a membership company | **Accepted.** The last recommendation priced a community. That is a product, not a company. |
| People join communities; they buy solutions. The trigger is the work (“we have £250k to spend”), not belonging | **Accepted.** |
| Suppliers will pay, because 10 Heads of L&D currently redesigning leadership is worth more than a 250-person conference logo | **Half right.** Suppliers pay for **access and pipeline**, not for “independent intelligence”. That is already a market. Entering it honestly is Option B. Entering it while claiming Option C is how trust dies. |

The line in the Corner report that should be kept:

> The product survives because the same 10 people keep meeting.

Keep that as the **moat**. Stop treating it as the **P&L**.

## 2. Where value is created — and who actually pays

The four sources of value are real. They are not one business.

| Value | Job to be done | Who historically pays | What year-1 Four Corners can sell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Peer intelligence | What are other organisations doing? | Members **or** sponsors who sit in the room | A Corner panel. Weak as the only revenue line at Head-of-L&D prices |
| 2. Decision confidence | Am I making the right investment? | The **buyer**, when a live spend exists | A project: Programme Decision Review |
| 3. Supplier intelligence | Who is genuinely good? | Buyers for independence; **vendors also pay** for briefings and grids | Not a 9-Grid. A narrow leadership-programme shortlist only after evidence exists |
| 4. Implementation learning | What happened afterwards? | Rarely as a standalone SKU. Vendor case studies are marketing. Independent archives take years | Capture from day 1. Do not sell “200 interviews” until ~30 exist |

A fifth source was missing from that list, and it is the one suppliers already buy:

| 5. Access | Time with people who control spend | **Suppliers** | Hosted dinners, 1:1s, in-room participation. This is a different company. |

Full table: `data/value_creation_vs_payer.csv`.

### Verified facts (not conference-sponsorship folklore)

**Gartner, FY2025 10-K (verified):** total revenue **$6.50bn**. Insights (subscription research) **$5.07bn (78%)**. Conferences **$645m (10%)**. Consulting **$552m (8.5%)**. Other **$227m**.

Even Gartner — the firm invoked as “research plus exchanges plus events” — has **one engine**. Events are distribution. Consulting is overflow. Insights is the company.

**Josh Bersin (his own LinkedIn, Oct 2024, verified as a founder statement):** corporate membership “starts around **$50,000** a year”. Separate vendor membership exists. Individual Galileo Professional is $39/month or $400/year — a side door, not the engine.

**Fosway (verified from fosway.com):** two paying sides. Corporates buy engagement contracts and (from 2024) individual membership (Professional / Premium / free Associate). Vendors buy a Vendor Programme and a Plus+ confidential advisory track. **Fees unpublished.** Core vendor research briefings are described as a zero-cost programme; paid programmes sit on top. Independence is the *claim*; dual revenue is the *structure*. 30 years of data sits underneath the 9-Grid.

**RedThread Research (verified, redthreadresearch.com):** boutique human-capital research. Professional membership **$800/year**. Enterprise “contact us”. Founded 2018. LinkedIn company page states **6 employees**. This is the realistic scale of a research boutique, not Gartner.

**Insight222 (verified):** People Analytics Program for **100+** member organisations; 40+ events/year; buyer-paid subscription (terms of business). **Fee unpublished.** Closest UK specialist analogue of “peer programme that exists to produce and sell intelligence”, not a generic HR community.

**iVentiv (verified):** members attend free if they pass the 10,000+ employee / VP-Director bar. Sponsors sit in the conversation and get 1:1s. HEC Paris has sponsored **88 events since 2010**, extended through 2026, and states it has **won significant contracts** from those rooms. HU-X’s published case-study teaser quotes the founder: *“The last two weeks have been incredibly busy. And that’s all business from iVentiv.”* Sponsor **fees unpublished**.

**Gordon Glenister executive dinners (verified list price):** **£10,000 + VAT** for one sponsor, 10–12 curated guests, London venue, moderation, white paper, and follow-up introductions to non-attendees. Past sponsors “have seen £100k+ in annual sales value following a single dinner” — **marketing claim, not audited**.

**HR Insight Forum (verified testimonials, price unpublished):** a partner describes an “informal networking supper with **15 HR Directors**” where they briefed the profiles they wanted. That is access sold as hospitality.

**Towards Maturity (verified history):** independent L&D benchmark, run as a CIC for ~11 years, then absorbed into Emerald Works with Mind Tools and GoodPractice (2020). The “free independent archive” did not remain a standalone commercial engine.

**Premier Partnership / Now Training (verified claims, not financials):** MLS is a supply-chain and administration business (Premier: 800+ partners, 400k learners claimed). It is not an intelligence firm. Kill for Four Corners.

### Calculated

Gartner Insights share: 5,072.6 / 6,497.2 = **78.1%**. Conferences **9.9%**. Consulting **8.5%**.

### Assumption

A serious 45-minute implementation interview plus a usable write-up takes **3–4 founder hours**. **200 interviews ≈ 600–800 hours ≈ 20–25 founder-weeks**, before any sales. **Unknown** whether anyone will pay for the 31st interview before the 200th exists.

### Unknown (do not invent)

- What iVentiv, Fosway, Evanta, Insight222 or HR Insight Forum actually charge
- What a UK leadership-development provider will pay Four Corners, specifically
- How many Heads of L&D in reach of EC1 have a live programme decision in the next 12 months
- Whether those Heads will pay a project fee rather than take a free hosted dinner from a provider

## 3. What I got wrong last time

The Corner report was right to kill iVentiv-for-the-next-tier as a **copy**, and right that CIPD’s £199–£261 sets a psychological ceiling for “professional membership”.

It was wrong in three ways.

**Wrong 1 — Membership as the company.**  
Pricing £1,200/year Corners made Four Corners a small Vistage for people who are not CEOs. That can exist. It is not the interesting opportunity, and it is not what the live site is already drifting toward (complimentary gathering, practitioner guests, pitch-free claim).

**Wrong 2 — Treating supplier demand as conference sponsorship.**  
SHRM roundtables at **$35,000** with badge scans were used as the comparable. That is what *conference* sponsors buy. Programme providers buy something else: **time with named buyers, repeated**. iVentiv, Glenister, HR Insight Forum and Forum Events already sell that. The last report said “sponsors won’t pay”. More accurate: **sponsors will not pay for a logo on a room they cannot enter, a list they cannot have, and an insight report from 12 people.** They will pay for access. The Glenister list price is the existence proof.

**Wrong 3 — Deferring “the work”.**  
Programme reviews, shortlists and lessons-learned were parked as later products. If the buying trigger is the work, parking the work is parking the business.

## 4. What the critique gets wrong

### 4.1 Option C is not an economic engine

The critique ends with the right question — *what is the single economic engine?* — then answers it with **eight products**:

Corners, interviews, programme reviews, buying guides, independent shortlists, research, procurement support, quarterly events, plus “strategic memberships”.

That is a **holding company after a decade**, not a launch. No analogue started there.

- Gartner: 78% one product family after forty years.
- Fosway: 30 years before the 9-Grid is a market object.
- Bersin: 26 years; engine still named “corporate membership”.
- RedThread: eight years, six people, $800 professional tier.
- Towards Maturity: eleven years as a CIC, then folded into a content business.

Calling this “Independent Learning Intelligence” does not make the eight SKUs affordable. It is the same kitchen-sink list as the first Four Corners brief, with better language.

**Kill Option C as a year-1 operating model.** Keep it as a map of *possible* later products **if** one engine compounds.

### 4.2 “10 Heads control millions, therefore they will pay / therefore suppliers will pay Four Corners”

Collective budget in a room is not a price. A provider pays Four Corners only if Four Corners is the **scarce path** to those people or to truth about them.

There are already paths:

- iVentiv, if the buyer is VP-level at 10,000+ employees
- Learning Technologies, CIPD Festival of Work — noisy, but cheap to stand
- Hosted dinners at ~£10k (Glenister public price)
- Direct BD; many leadership providers are built on founder relationships
- Analyst briefings (Fosway, Gartner, Bersin) if the category is HR tech more than leadership programmes

Four Corners is scarce only if the **same 10 people keep meeting** *and* Four Corners uniquely captures what they will not tell a vendor. That is an insight asset. It is not automatically a £10k access product. If you sell it as access, you are Glenister with an L&D niche. If you refuse access, you must sell insight or decision work, and those prices are unproven.

### 4.3 “People don’t buy communities” does not mean “don’t have a recurring engine”

Gartner’s buyers are not buying a community. They are buying cover for a decision. They still pay **every year**. Recurring revenue is not the same as belonging.

The mistake is selling belonging. The mistake in the other direction is running a project shop with no compounding asset. Projects without a panel are ordinary consultancy. A panel without a paid job is a club.

### 4.4 200 implementation interviews are an asset, not a business

Agreed they would be valuable. Agreed they are not testimonials.

They fail as a *plan* because:

1. Production cost is a founder-year (assumption above).
2. Towards Maturity shows how long a genuine independent benchmark takes, and how it still struggled to remain a standalone firm.
3. Vendors will offer to fund interviews about *their* programmes. That is a case-study mill. Buyers will discount it.
4. The 12th interview is useful internally. The 200th is a product. There is a desert in between where you have costs and no SKU.

**Do capture structured lessons-learned from every Corner from day 1.** Do not build a “library” product, a website of reviews, or a promise of 200.

## 5. Three directions — plus the one that was smuggled inside C

| Option | Engine | Corners are | Independence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Community** | Member dues | The product | High if rooms stay clean |
| **B. Access** | Supplier fees for time with buyers | Bait | Low. Guests know why they were invited |
| **C. Intelligence catalogue** | Many | One of many | Confused |
| **D. Decision engine** | Buyer project fees on live spends; later, nameless supplier insight | The factory | High on buyer files |

Option B is the commercially easiest. It is also the one the live Four Corners language (“pitch-free”, “independent”) forbids unless James is willing to change the claim.

Option D is the only way to mean “independent learning intelligence” *and* have a cheque in year 1 that is not a community subscription.

## 6. Kill-test of Option D

**Could it fail?** Yes. Ways it dies:

1. **No live decisions in the network.** If the 25 people you can actually reach are not redesigning leadership this year, there is no project pipeline. Then D is a slogan.
2. **HRD / procurement will not buy a £4–8k critical-friend review** because Fosway/Gartner already sit on the tech side, and leadership programmes are bought on chemistry and a preferred supplier. **Unknown.**
3. **Providers offer the Head a free diagnostic** (they already do). Four Corners’ only advantage is not wanting the delivery contract. That advantage only matters if the Head *feels* the conflict. Many do not.
4. **Corners refuse to be a research panel.** People will talk in the room and refuse write-up. Then there is no compounding asset.
5. **James takes a £10k hosted-dinner cheque** because it is easier than selling reviews. The brand becomes B while the website says C. That is the most likely quiet failure.

**Why it can survive**

- The buying trigger is real: leadership and manager-capability programmes are large, recurring, poorly evidenced spends. (Magnitude of a *specific* £250k is an **example**, not a verified average.)
- Independence is scarce in leadership development in a way it is not in HR tech (Fosway/Gartner already occupy tech). The white space is **programmes, not platforms**.
- Intact Corners produce the one input buyers cannot get from a provider pitch: *what happened in organisations like mine*.
- Supplier money can come later as **aggregated Voice of Buyer**, which is how Fosway and Gartner sell to vendors without putting them in the buyer’s project.

## 7. Recommended company

### Identity (what you say)

**Four Corners helps in-house L&D, leadership and talent teams make better learning investment decisions.**

Not: a membership community.  
Not: a conference.  
Not: an MLS.  
Not: “Gartner for L&D” (you do not have 2,400 analysts).

### Economic engine (what you invoice in the next 18 months)

**Primary cash engine: Programme Decision Reviews.**

A defined piece of work, sold when an organisation is about to choose, redesign or re-procure a material learning investment (leadership, manager capability, or a similarly concentrated programme).

**Assumption of a test price, not a market price:** £4,000–£8,000 per review (roughly 4–8 founder days). Higher only after two paid reference cases. Not £8–15k “membership”. Not £1,200 “community”.

What the buyer actually receives:

- Structured interviews with their team
- Anonymised input from the Corner panel (“what peers would ask before signing”)
- A short written recommendation they can take to their HRD
- Explicit non-recommendation of named suppliers unless the evidence file supports it
- No delivery of the programme itself. Ever.

**Corners:** the factory. Same 10 people, 6 times a year, London + online as already specified. Members are **contributors**, not the P&L. Charge a **token** (£0–£400/year) only to reduce no-shows — or require a lessons-learned write-up as the price of the seat. Do not build the company around that fee.

**Implementation interviews:** a **method**, not a product. Every Corner produces 1–2 structured “what we tried / what failed / six months later” notes, named only with consent. Internal corpus first.

**Supplier revenue, year 1:** none required. Year 2, if 20+ cases exist: **Voice of Buyer briefings** (category-level, anonymised, no introductions, no attendee lists). If a provider will only buy names or a seat, that is Option B. Decline or relabel the company.

**Events:** one quarterly gathering as distribution and pipeline for reviews, not as the engine. If a gathering is sponsored, the sponsor is **outside the Corner**, disclosed, and does not receive the list. Price that against Glenister (£10k) only if you are willing to admit it is access. Otherwise do not sell it.

**Large-event strategy:** secondary. Do not build a 100-person conference to fund intelligence you have not written.

### What this is not

| Kill | Why |
|---|---|
| MLS / preferred-supplier marketplace | Premier and Now already exist. You become a channel. Independence ends. |
| Eight-product website | Option C launch fantasy |
| In-Corner suppliers | That is iVentiv/Glenister. Pick B explicitly if you want it |
| Public programme reviews / star ratings | Defamation, thin n, vendor capture |
| “200 interview archive” as a year-1 promise | Production and chicken-egg |
| Four function brands | Same as last report |
| £8–15k membership as the plan | Wrong object |

### What to build now

1. Independence charter (who pays, what they cannot buy).
2. One L&D Corner of 10, run as a **research panel** with a written capture template.
3. One Decision Review offer on a single page. No other SKUs.
4. A lessons-learned template used at every meeting.
5. A named list of 40 Heads and which of them have a live programme decision (this is the real CRM).

### What to defer

Talent/HR/OD brands; Manchester; app; buying guides; shortlists as a product; supplier briefings until n≥20; Chair franchise.

### Year-1 numbers (illustrative, not a forecast)

**Assumption:** 3 Corners running; 4 paid Decision Reviews close; 0–1 gathering sponsor that is *not* Option B in disguise.

| Line | Low | High |
|---|---:|---:|
| Decision Reviews (3–6 × £4–8k) | £12,000 | £48,000 |
| Token Corner seats (if used) | £0 | £10,000 |
| Gathering sponsor (optional, disclosed, no list) | £0 | £8,000 |
| **Total** | **£12,000** | **£66,000** |

This is still a specialist practice. It is a **better** practice than a £1,200 club, because each invoice is attached to a buying moment and each Corner meeting adds to a file that can later be sold as insight.

It does not become a firm until reviews repeat and the corpus is cited by people who were not in the room.

## 8. The line you cannot sit on

You can be **independent intelligence** (buyers pay; suppliers buy only aggregated truth).

Or you can be **access** (suppliers pay to meet the 10 people; Glenister/iVentiv/HRIF).

You cannot be both in year 1 without lying to one side.

The Glenister £10k dinner is the temptation. It is also the test:

> If you would take £10,000 next month to fill a private room for a leadership provider, you are building Option B. Say so. Stop using “independent”.

> If you would refuse that cheque, you are building Option D. Then you must sell Decision Reviews, because token membership will not feed you.

There is no clever hybrid that keeps the £10k and the Fosway-like reputation. Fosway’s vendor money sits on **research process and grids**, after decades, not on a dinner list.

## 10. 30-day validation (rewritten for the engine question)

Do **not** ask “would you join a community?”  
Do **not** debate £1,200 vs £750.

### Week 1–2 — 25 buyer conversations

Of which at least 15 are in-house Heads/Directors of L&D, Leadership or Talent.

Ask, in this order:

1. What learning investment over ~£50k are you making or remaking in the next 12 months? (If none, they are not a D customer this year. They may still be a panel member.)
2. Who else is in that decision? What would make you more confident before you sign?
3. Last time you bought, what did you actually rely on (peer call, incumbent, analyst, RFP, chemistry)?
4. Would your organisation pay **£5,000** for a two-week independent review that includes anonymised peer challenge and a written note for your HRD — yes, no, or only if free?
5. Would you sit in a 10-person Corner six times a year if the price of the seat was a written lessons-learned, not a fee?

### Week 1–2 — 10 provider conversations (leadership, coaching, assessment, not LMS)

Show **three** products. Ask them to put a number on each, or to refuse.

| Product | What they get | What they do not get |
|---|---|---|
| **B — Hosted access** | 10 named Heads, one afternoon/dinner, they may attend, follow-up allowed | Independence claim |
| **D-insight — Voice of Buyer** | 8–12 page anonymised synthesis from a Corner on a stated topic | Names, intros, seat, list |
| **Logo** | Acknowledgement on a gathering | Everything else |

Record the number they name. Do not prompt with Glenister’s £10k until they have answered.

### Week 3 — two live tests

- **Panel test:** one 90-minute online Corner, 8–10 people, capture template used in the room. Success = 7 stay and at least **5 written lessons-learned** exist the next morning.
- **Offer test:** send the Decision Review one-pager to everyone who answered “yes” or “maybe” to the £5k question.

### Pass / fail (logic)

Denominators are small because this is a relationship market. Thresholds exist so that *failure is cheaper than a year of mixed messaging*.

1. **Live-buy density:** of 15 qualified Heads, **fewer than 5** have a material learning decision in 12 months → Option D has no pipeline. Do not build a decision-support firm on a dormant network.
2. **Buyer WTP:** of those with a live decision, **fewer than 2** will take a £5k review to their HRD (calendar + “I’ll ask”) → D fails as cash engine. Then only B or stop.
3. **Provider WTP for B:** **3 or more of 10** name **≥ £8,000** for hosted access → access is a real business. If you also failed test 2, the honest move is Option B or exit. If you passed test 2, **refuse B** or you will cannibalise D.
4. **Provider WTP for insight without names:** **fewer than 2 of 10** name **≥ £2,000** for a nameless synthesis → do not plan year-1 supplier research revenue. The archive is for buyers first.
5. **Logo-only:** if the only numbers you hear are for B, and they collapse when names/seat are removed, the last report was right about branding and the critique was right about access. Those are different offers.
6. **Panel capture:** fewer than 5 usable write-ups from the pilot Corner → you do not have a compounding asset. Facilitation is theatre.
7. **The Glenister test:** if James would accept a B cheque this month, stop writing D documents.

£5k and £8k are **test anchors**, not proven prices. £5k is inside a 4–8 day critical-friend job (assumption). £8k is just below Glenister’s public £10k+VAT, so a provider who will pay Glenister but not Four Corners is saying the *guest list and follow-up*, not the topic, is what they buy.

## 12. Questions for James

Ranked by how much the answer changes the engine. Not knowable from desks.

1. **Would you refuse a £10,000 cheque next month to host 10 Heads of L&D for a named leadership provider, with follow-up allowed?** If no, you are Option B. The rest of this memo is decoration.
2. **Can you name, this week, 10 organisations in your reach that will make or remake a >£50k learning programme in the next 12 months?** If no, Option D has no pipeline and you are back to a club.
3. **Will Four Corners ever deliver, white-label, or recommend a paid programme it has a commercial interest in?** A single “sometimes” ends independence.
4. **Are consultants in the Corner or not?** A panel of in-house practitioners is an intelligence asset. A mixed room is a networking asset. Different companies.
5. **Is year-3 success a £80–150k specialist practice, a saleable research boutique, or a sponsor platform?** D can become the second. B is the third. A is the first. They hire differently.
6. **Who is paying for 25 August — you, a guest’s firm, or a provider?** If a provider, the engine is already B.
7. **Will you invoice for a Decision Review before the “community feels ready”?** Waiting for readiness is how A returns through the back door.
8. **What relationship exists with iVentiv beyond Charles Jennings as a guest?** Shared chairs help content and make “we are not a supplier room” harder to argue.
9. **If 5 of 15 Heads have a live buy and 0 will pay £5k, but 4 providers will pay £10k for access, do you take the access money?** This is question 1 with data.
10. **Who writes the lessons-learned — you after every Corner, or the member as the price of the seat?** If you will not enforce capture, there is no archive, and C’s favourite asset does not exist.
