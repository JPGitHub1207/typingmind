## 2. Honest verdict on the three you named

### 2.1 FE consulting

Real, and crowded.

Quality / Ofsted / SAR-QIP is a mature cottage industry of **former inspectors and quality directors** (Best for Training, Brian Cairns Consulting, many independents). **Verified** as existing offers; their win-rates and prices are **unknown**.

Commercial / employer-engagement consulting is also occupied. **FE Business** publicly positions as “the market leader in employer engagement consultancy and training for the skills sector” and sells transformation projects, sales training, interim management and outsourced BD. **Verified**, febusiness.co.uk, retrieved 14 August 2026. Appointment-led lead generation for apprenticeship providers is sold by firms such as **Projects Marketing** and **Lead Gen Specialists**. **Verified** as offers.

**Implication:** generic “FE consultant” is a job, not a company. You only win if the offer is narrower than “I know FE.”

A workable narrow version: **retained commercial operator for independent training providers that are too small for a FE Business transformation project and too operational for a lead-gen agency.** You sit in the business one or two days a week: pipeline, email, events, bids, sales team, quality of the commercial process. That is the directorship skill, productised.

Colleges as the primary buyer: **low priority**. Slow procurement, relationship-led, often want ex-principals or ex-HMI. Take a college if it phones you. Do not build the go-to-market around colleges.

### 2.2 Relaunch the Work Based Learning Alliance

**Do not relaunch it as a membership / lobbying body.**

AELP already is the national membership organisation for independent training providers, employer providers, colleges and adjacent bodies. Published full-member rates (learners on funded programmes) run from **£700** (up to 100 learners) to **£12,150** (5,000+), plus VAT, with a slightly different published set on the “full membership” page. **Verified**, aelp.org.uk membership pages, retrieved 14 August 2026. They already sell lobbying, briefings, events, SIGs and model contracts.

Prior UK WBLA-named companies were dissolved (Companies House: London Work Based Learning Alliance 05003899 dissolved 30 April 2019; Work Based Learning Alliance Ltd 05983819 dissolved 13 October 2020). **Verified**. The live “Work-Based Learning Alliance” brand online is a **US nonprofit** — unrelated. **Verified**.

Your own observation still holds: membership clubs are often poorly run and still succeed. That argues for a **small, tightly run, paid commercial peer board or event series**, not for rebuilding a sector alliance with no policy voice, no conference machine, and AELP already collecting the subs.

If eight named ITP MDs would pay for a confidential commercial board, that is idea 7 below. Call it something new. Do not resurrect WBLA.

### 2.3 Levy employers as employer-providers

Treat “become an employer-provider” as a **rare, high-cost outcome**, not the headline.

To train their own staff on levy funds, a levy-paying employer must join the **Apprenticeship Provider and Assessment Register (APAR)** on the employer-provider route, have traded 12 months, be able to start training within six months, and have a management structure experienced in developing and delivering training. Applications are limited to once per 12 months. Employer-providers deliver on an **actual cost** basis to own / connected-company staff. **Verified**, GOV.UK APAR guidance and August 2025 APAR application guidance.

That is a 12–24 month infrastructure project (policies, delivery model, ILR, funding rules, Ofsted exposure), not a workshop.

The APAR-application market is already served by specialists, including **Strategic Development Network** (led by former ESFA senior managers), **Skills Office Network**, **ESS Assist**, and **DSG L&M Consultancy**. **Verified** as public offers.

Meanwhile the **Growth and Skills Levy** rules from **1 August 2026** are a genuine planning shock for levy HR: new funds expire after **12 months** not 24; the **10% government top-up stops** on new inflows; co-investment when a levy account is empty is **25% / 75%** for new starts. Product families are approved apprenticeships, foundation apprenticeships and named apprenticeship units — not generic short courses. AELP has cited **more than £3.3 billion** of levy funds returned to the Treasury since 2019 and around **48%** of levy-paying businesses reporting returned unspent funds each year. **Verified** as AELP’s published figures, not re-audited here.

**The honest product is therefore:**

1. Should this employer become a provider at all? (Usually no.)
2. If no: a 12-month spend / transfer / units plan that is **independent of any training provider**.
3. If yes: you are competing with SDN et al on APAR, which is a worse use of your sales-and-events stack.

Every apprenticeship provider in the country is currently emailing levy employers with “don’t waste your levy.” Independence is the only wedge. If you later deliver training, you have spent the wedge.

## 3. How to generate ideas that fit you

Filter: **someone already has a budget, the scarce thing is commercial judgement or a commercial engine, you can charge a recurring fee, and AI/online makes you faster rather than turning you into a software company.**

Discard: anything whose scarce thing is “I can find trainers / coaches / speakers.”

Three buyer types worth designing for:

1. **Independent training-provider owners** — they can deliver; they cannot consistently sell, bid, run events, or manage a sales team. They pay from trading income.
2. **Levy employers (HR / L&D / finance)** — they have a pot that now expires faster. They will pay for an independent plan. They should not usually pay you to become a provider.
3. **Vendors who need a room of providers** — awarding bodies, EPAOs, LMS / ILR / e-portfolio vendors. They pay for events, lists and BD. This can be an online-heavy business. It fails the independence test if you also advise those providers on which vendor to pick.

## 8. 15-conversation script (use this week)

Do not pitch a brand. Ask:

1. What is actually hard in the next 12 months — selling, funding rules, quality, cash, or hiring?
2. What have you already paid for that did not work (AELP, a consultant, a lead-gen firm, a coach)?

5. Who else should I be speaking to?

Stop at 15 conversations or four weeks, whichever comes first. Count how many times they describe **pipeline / sales team / bids / events** versus **quality / Ofsted** versus **levy confusion**. The majority answer is the business. If they mostly want cheap trainers, you already know that is not yours.
